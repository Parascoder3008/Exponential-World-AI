# The Board Review

Eight operators' frameworks applied to this business, plus Dan Martell's on the delegation design. Run against the real numbers: **₹1–5L/month, 10–100K subscribers, 3–6 months runway, both offers proven cold.**

They converged. That is the notable part.

---

## The consensus diagnosis

> **This is not a systems problem. It is a harvest problem.**

An under-monetised audience and two proven cold offers are sitting idle while the plan proposes 90 days of infrastructure work. At 3–6 months of runway, that is the most expensive available move.

Nobody on the board disputed the AI OS. Every one of them disputed doing it first.

---

## Hormozi — offer and pricing

**The call:** you built a ₹5,000 offer for an audience that can buy a ₹1,00,000 offer.

The Value Equation holds on Dream Outcome and Perceived Likelihood — the proof is real and the demo runs live. It breaks on price anchoring. **A ₹5,000 product with Nippon India Mutual Fund on the CV is a positioning error, not a pricing one.** Low price signals low stakes and attracts the buyers who complain most.

Second failure: **no ascension.** A bootcamp buyer finishes and has nowhere to go. All the LTV leaks out of the top of a ladder with one rung.

Third: *"the riches are in the niches."* Two audiences is the split-focus killer. One thing until it produces more than you can handle.

**Fix:** rungs above ₹5,000 before anything else. A named guarantee. Real scarcity beyond bonus limits.

---

## Brunson — funnel

**The call:** you already own a Perfect Webinar and you're not running it.

Forty-six slides, eight blocks, six poll beats, demo-before-offer, value stack, fast-action bonuses. It converts cold. The research says interactive polls lift CTA clicks up to 3× — **the mechanism the benchmarks credit is already built into the deck.**

Two problems: it runs occasionally rather than on a schedule, and it terminates at ₹5,000 with no Value Ladder above it.

**Fix:** run it on a fixed cadence, build the ladder above it, and treat every past attendee who didn't buy as inventory.

---

## Godin — market

**The call:** two audiences is two businesses.

Retail traders and SMB owners share nothing — not the problem, not the worldview, not the buying trigger, not the language. Serving both means the Smallest Viable Market is undefined, and an undefined market cannot spread anything by word of mouth.

On the Purple Cow question: "business person teaching AI" is good positioning but not remarkable on its own. **Building an AI operating system in public, on your own business, twice a week — that is the remarkable thing.** Lead with it.

**Fix:** choose. Then make the build-in-public series the signature.

---

## Kennedy — message-market-media

**The call:** the break is at *Market*, so no message optimisation will help.

Also: you're marketing to affluent buyers with non-affluent pricing. SMB owners with real budget want done-for-you and a named methodology, not a cheap course. **The 4C Framework should be the product name, not internal jargon** — it makes the offer non-comparable, which is the whole point of proprietary methodology.

---

## Gary Vee — volume and content

**The call:** 10–100K subs at ₹1–5L/month means you've been all jabs and almost no right hooks. The audience has been trained that you don't sell.

He'd validate build-in-public hard — document, don't create — and the research agrees (14.6% vs 1.7% inbound conversion). But volume without the ask is a hobby.

**One addition from the research he'd take:** teams with 5+ leadership voices posting get 3–5× the organic reach of a single founder. Get the team posting.

---

## Cardone — activity

**The call:** how many times last month did you actually ask someone for money?

That number is the real revenue ceiling, and at ₹1–5L it is almost certainly single digits. Also the follow-up point: most people quit after two or three touches. **Every past webinar attendee who didn't buy is free pipeline that has already raised its hand.**

---

## Belfort — certainty

**The call:** the US-first market order attacks your weakest certainty first.

The three certainties are product, you, and company. In India and UAE all three are at ten — real logos, real referrals, timezone overlap, language. In the US, company certainty is near zero because nobody has heard of Nippon India MF.

**Fix:** win where certainty is highest, build US case studies from there, bridge later. This directly reverses the v1 market order.

---

## Robbins — decision

**The call:** you are running two businesses, building a third, and planning a fourth.

Decision means cutting off alternatives. The plan's own risk register says thirty-eight agents at four people is how nothing ships — the same logic applies one level up, to the businesses themselves.

---

## Martell — the delegation design

Not in the original panel; added because the human-layer design is his territory.

**Buyback Loop — Audit → Transfer → Fill.** The plan does Audit and Transfer well: four lanes, named inputs and outputs, SLAs. It fails at **Fill**. Time bought back is being spent *building agents*, when the highest-value use is *selling*. Buying back time only counts if the reclaimed hours go to the highest-value work.

**Replacement Ladder — Admin → Delivery → Marketing → Sales → Leadership.** The hires cover delivery (editor), marketing (ads), and some admin (VA). Correctly, sales is retained — the buyer is buying Paras. But the ladder is being climbed while the founder's reclaimed time goes into engineering rather than revenue.

**10-80-10 — founder does the first 10% and the last 10%, the team does the 80%.** This is exactly what the Editor Brief agent implements: Paras specifies the hook and approves the cut, the editor does the middle. Extend the same pattern to every lane.

## What the board did *not* challenge

Worth recording, because it means these survived scrutiny:

- The AI OS architecture and the nine-agent build order
- Build-once-sell-twice, and the 30-day rule before anything is sold
- The four human lanes and the OS Operator role
- Build-in-public as the primary GTM
- Content → Commerce → Money → Product as the phase order **after** cash is secured
- The 4C Framework itself — Kennedy would put it *on the price tag*

---

## The resulting change

One insertion, one reversal, one decision:

1. **Insert** a 30-day cash sprint before Phase 1. Everything else shifts back a month.
2. **Reverse** the market order to India/UAE first.
3. **Decide** the audience: SMB AI is the business; trading funds the sprint, then goes on maintenance.
