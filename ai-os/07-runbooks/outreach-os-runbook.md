# Outreach OS — The Runbook

**What this is:** the repeatable loop that turns a name into a paid engagement. It is the front half of Commerce OS, run manually in Sprint 0 and automated in Phase 2.

**Two motions, and they are not the same job:**

| | **Motion 1 — Warm** | **Motion 2 — Cold** |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Past clients, referrals, warm network, workshop attendees | Sourced strangers |
| Market | India + UAE | US first |
| Sells | 4C Workshop ₹1.5–4L → Programme ₹8–25L | Free 4C Audit → Install |
| Cycle | **1–3 weeks** | 3–8 weeks |
| Time | 90 min/day | 30 min/day |
| Job in Sprint 0 | **This month's cash** | Next month's pipeline |

**Motion 1 is where September's money is.** Motion 2 keeps running because stopping it costs you October.

---

## Motion 1 — The warm loop

```
  LIST ──▶ RESEARCH ──▶ REACH OUT ──▶ FOLLOW UP ──▶ CALL ──▶ PROPOSE ──▶ CLOSE ──▶ REFER
   D1        per name      D2+          0/3/10/15    ~D7      ~D14       ~D21     always
```

**The rule that produces most of the revenue:** ask every single conversation for a referral, whether it closed or not. Referrals are the highest-converting source available and the most under-asked.

---

### Step 1 · LIST

**Owner** Paras · **When** Day 1 · **Time** 2 hrs · **Target 30 names**

Four tiers, in this order — approach tier 1 before tier 4, always:

| Tier | Who | Why first |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Past clients — Nippon, Pluto Travels, Wingsway, Ahuja, Raizing, Jaypore | They already bought. Highest certainty on all three axes. |
| **2** | Referrals from tier 1 | Highest conversion of any source |
| **3** | Warm network — workshop attendees, event contacts, repeat LinkedIn engagers | They know the name |
| **4** | Wednesday live-session attendees | Enterprise buyers self-qualify by showing up |

**Columns:** name · company · how you know them · last contact · which offer fits · next action + date.

**Where it lives:** Google Sheets while cold, GHL once they reply. **Do not fill GHL with people who haven't answered** — a CRM full of strangers is a CRM nobody opens.

### Step 2 · RESEARCH each name

**Owner** Paras · **Time** 15 min per name

```
/paras-lead-engine
```
Paste: the company name and site, and note that this is a **warm** contact — past client or referral — so the credibility bridge is not needed.

**You get back:** verification the company is real and active, the 4C diagnostic scored /100 with evidence, an Opportunity score /10, their AI OS stage, and the named primary gap.

**The one rule that overrides everything: never write to a company you have not verified with a tool this session.** Emailing an invented detail destroys the thing you are actually selling, which is credibility.

**Stage decides the offer:**

| Their stage | What you observe | What you offer |
|---|---|---|
| **learn** | No AI anywhere, curious at most | 4C Workshop |
| **earn** | ChatGPT used ad hoc, no system | 4C Workshop → Install |
| **help** | Systems exist, want to scale | 4C Install |
| **train** | Scaling, needs org-wide capability | 4C Programme |
| **invest** | Mature, seeking leverage | 4C Partner |

### Step 3 · REACH OUT

**Owner** Paras · **When** from Day 2 · **Time** 90 min/day · **Volume** 5 sends/day

The cold templates in `paras-lead-engine/references/outreach.md` are built for strangers and carry the US credibility bridge. **Warm contacts need none of that** — they already know who you are, and using a cold template on a past client reads as a mail-merge.

**Voice rules carry over unchanged:** under 120 words · one specific observation · no adjectives about your own service · one ask, never two · 8th-grade reading level · lowercase specific subject line · sign as Paras.

#### Template A — Past client (tier 1)

> **subject:** what's changed at [Company] since [month/year]
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> We ran the [specific thing — the 2-day AI programme / the ads rebuild] back in [when]. I've been thinking about what that would look like now — the tooling has moved a long way in [N] months.
>
> Two things I'd do differently if we started today: [specific 1], and [specific 2].
>
> Are you and the team still working the way you were then, or has that shifted?
>
> — Paras

The ask is a question about *them*, not a pitch. It restarts a real conversation. The offer comes on the reply.

#### Template B — Referral (tier 2)

> **subject:** [Referrer] suggested I write
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> [Referrer] mentioned you're working out where AI actually fits at [Company] — and that it's less obvious than the internet makes it sound.
>
> I run AI training for teams. Last month that was [specific: a 2-day programme for a mutual fund's marketing team]. Most of it is unlearning what people think it does.
>
> I put together a short read on where AI usually pays off in [their industry] — four pages, specific. Want me to send it over?
>
> — Paras

The referrer's name is in the subject because it is the only thing that matters at this moment.

#### Template C — Warm network / workshop attendee (tiers 3–4)

> **subject:** you were at Wednesday's session
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> You've been at a few of the Wednesday sessions — thanks for that.
>
> The question that keeps coming up from people running teams your size is [specific recurring question]. I've started answering it properly as a one-day workshop for the whole team rather than one person at a time.
>
> Would that be useful at [Company], or is it not where you are right now?
>
> — Paras

"Or is it not where you are right now" gives permission to say no, which is what makes people answer.

**The test before anything sends:** could this have gone to any other company? If yes, it is not finished. Delete the reusable parts and keep only what is true of them alone.

---

### Step 4 · FOLLOW UP

**Owner** Paras · **Cadence** Day 0 → 3 → 10 → 15, set the moment the first touch sends

**Every follow-up adds something new.** "Just bumping this" trains people to ignore you.

| Touch | Day | Job |
|---|---|---|
| First | 0 | Observation + the ask |
| FU 1 | 3 | **Add value, don't re-ask.** A second finding, or how a comparable business handled it. |
| FU 2 | 10 | **Handle the objection.** Name the likely reason for silence — usually "not ready" or "no bandwidth" — and answer it in two lines. |
| Breakup | 15 | **Close the loop.** "I'll stop here. Happy to send the breakdown either way — say the word." |

**The breakup has the highest reply rate of the three, and it only works because it's true.** If you drafted the audit, it genuinely is sitting there. Don't use the line if it isn't.

**Most people stop at three touches. Most revenue is past it.** Anyone at three with no reply gets a fourth.

Any reply stops the sequence immediately and a human takes over.

---

### Step 5 · THE CALL

**Owner** Paras · **~Day 7** · **30–45 min**

**Structure — diagnose before you prescribe:**

1. **What changed** (5 min) — why are they talking to you now
2. **Walk the 4C** (15 min) — take them through their own diagnostic. Show the evidence, quote their own material. This is the whole call.
3. **Name the biggest single gap** (5 min) — one, not four. Breadth reads as a sales pitch; one specific gap reads as a diagnosis.
4. **Match the offer to the stage** (10 min) — workshop, programme or install
5. **Agree the next step with a date** (5 min)

**Never leave without a dated next action.** "I'll get back to you" is a no with better manners.

---

### Step 6 · PROPOSE

**Owner** Paras · **~Day 14** · **Within 48h of the call, always**

**One page. Five parts:**

1. **What we found** — the 4C gap, in their words
2. **What we'll do** — the named engagement, with dates
3. **What you get** — deliverables, listed
4. **What it costs** — the number, from Brand Brain, not invented on the page
5. **What happens next** — one action, dated

Price from `ai-os/00-strategy/offer-ladder.md`: Workshop **₹1.5–4L**, Programme **₹8–25L**, Install **₹1.5–3L + ₹40–80K/mo**. US pricing 2–3× in dollars.

**Price against the headcount it replaces or the revenue it unlocks. Never against hours.** Hours invite comparison; outcomes don't.

---

### Step 7 · CLOSE, then REFER

At close and at loss, ask the same question:

> *"Who else in your network is trying to figure this out right now?"*

At close it is natural — they're happy. At loss it is disarming, and it often produces a better lead than the one you lost.

**Every closed engagement immediately becomes two things:** a delivery to run, and a case study to capture. Delivery that produces no proof asset is delivered at half value.

## The webinar sub-loop

Runs once inside Sprint 0 — **Wed 16 September**. The full schedule is in `../02-roadmap/30-day-detailed-plan.md`.

### Build it

```
/paras-webinar-builder
```
Paste: the SMB AI topic, the audience (Indian SMB owners and creators), and both CTAs — the ₹25–50K cohort, and the free 4C Audit.

**The 8 blocks, in order:** cold-open contrast hook → authority stack (one credential per slide) → exactly three promises → the 4C framework → **five-tool walkthrough, a poll after each** → live demo with a real result → the turn → offer with value stack.

**Three non-negotiables:**
- **Demo before offer, always.** A real prompt run and a real result before any price appears.
- **Six poll beats minimum.** Polls lift CTA clicks up to 3×. They are the retention mechanism, not decoration.
- **Two CTAs.** Benchmarks show two outperforms one — and here they route two different audiences.

### Run it

Tech check 60 min before. Replay out within the hour. Log the funnel: registered → attended → stayed to offer → bought → audit requests.

**Benchmarks to judge yourself against:** registration→live median **41.6%** (bottom quartile 28.4% — plan for that) · attendee→offer **5–12%** high-ticket, **15–25%** educational.

### Follow up — this is where the money is

Most people run the webinar and stop. **The 72 hours after produce more than the live call.**

| When | Action |
|---|---|
| T+1h | Replay to everyone, both CTAs |
| T+1d | **Segmented personal follow-up** — stayed-to-offer gets a different message than left-early |
| T+1d | **Every 4C Audit request scheduled.** These are the corporate pipeline. |
| T+2d | Replay to registrants who didn't attend |
| T+3d | Cart close, if the cohort has a dated start |
| T+7d | Encore for anyone who missed |

## Motion 2 — Cold, 30 min/day

Keeps running through Sprint 0 at low volume. It is not this month's cash; it is next month's pipeline.

**Weekly rhythm** (from `paras-lead-engine/references/pipeline.md`):

| Day | Work |
|---|---|
| Mon | Pick the week's niche + metro. Source and verify. 25–40 raw → 10–15 qualified. |
| Tue | Diagnose the qualified set. Draft audits for anyone scoring 7+. |
| Wed | Paras reviews and edits. First touches send. |
| Thu | LinkedIn requests to the same list. Send audits to anyone who replied. |
| Fri | Follow-ups due. Log replies. |

**Standing constraints:** Paras reviews every send, no exceptions · cap ~30/day on a warm domain · honour do-not-contact permanently, across every list, forever · **one niche at a time** — niche-hopping resets the compounding.

---

## Measure — the five numbers, every 50 attempts

Not weekly. Smaller samples say nothing.

1. **Reply rate by source tier** — which of the four warm tiers actually produces conversations
2. **Reply rate by vertical** — where the transferable proof actually transfers
3. **Audit acceptance rate** — % of replies taking the free 4C Audit
4. **Audit → call rate** — is the audit persuasive or merely polite
5. **Bounce rate** — **over 3% means sourcing drifted toward guesswork.** Stop and fix it before sending anything else.

**#1 matters more than it looks.** After 50 attempts it tells you which tier produces buyers rather than names, and that reshapes the whole engine.

Log every winner. Paste replying emails back into the templates. **This is the only part of the system that improves on its own — don't skip the logging.**

## What breaks this loop

| Symptom | Real cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| No replies from warm contacts | Cold template used on a warm name | Templates A–C above, rebuilt per person |
| Conversations stall after one call | No dated next action | Never end a call without one |
| Bounce rate over 3% | Sourcing drifted to guessing | Stop sending. Fix verification first. |
| Lots of calls, no proposals | Proposal not sent within 48h | Send it within 48h or the heat is gone |
| Pipeline dries up in month 2 | Motion 2 was paused for Motion 1 | 30 min/day, always |
| Referrals never appear | Nobody asked | Ask on every close *and* every loss |
| Content eats the outreach hour | Outreach scheduled second | Block 1 before Block 2, daily |
