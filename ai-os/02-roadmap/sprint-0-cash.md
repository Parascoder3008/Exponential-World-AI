# Sprint 0 — Cash (30 days)

**Goal:** buy the runway that funds everything else, using assets that already exist.

**Not the goal:** building anything. Two skills get written in the margins. Nothing else.

---

## Why this exists

At ₹1–5L/month with 3–6 months of runway, a monetisable audience, and two offers that have already sold cold, the binding constraint is not systems. **It is that nothing is being sold at the right price to people who already know you.**

The v1 plan opened with a month of foundation work. That is correct for a business at ₹5–20L/month. At these numbers it spends a fifth of the runway on infrastructure while proven offers sit idle.

Sprint 0 inverts it: **harvest first, build second, and let the harvest pay for the build.**

---

## The three tracks

Run concurrently. Track A is the highest value, Track B the fastest, Track C fills the gaps.

| Track | What | Why | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — Corporate training** | Sell the Nippon/Wingsway shape at benchmark prices, warm + referral, India & UAE | Highest ticket, fastest close, proof already exists | **1 engagement closed or contracted** |
| **B — Audience harvest** | Run the existing webinar to the existing audience; build rungs above ₹5,000; reactivate non-buyers | The asset is built and converts cold | **1 webinar run, ladder live** |
| **C — Foundation** | Brand Brain with real prices, four lanes, scorecard, tracking | Needed for Phase 1, cheap to do in margins | **~6 hrs/week** |

## Track A — Corporate training

**The insight this track rests on:** benchmark pricing for Indian corporate AI training is **₹1.5–4L for a one-day workshop (100 people)** and **₹8–25L for a multi-week role-specific programme** ([evidence](../06-evidence/market-research.md#7-pricing-benchmarks)).

The Nippon India Mutual Fund engagement was a two-day AI training programme. That is a ₹1.5–4L+ shape at benchmark. **One engagement equals a month or more of current revenue**, and the credibility to sell it already exists.

This is not the top of a funnel. It is the highest-ticket, fastest-closing item on the menu, and it has been mispriced as an afterthought.

**Who to approach, in this order:**

1. **Past clients** — Nippon, Pluto Travels, Wingsway, Ahuja, Raizing, Jaypore. They bought once. Ask what changed since, and offer the 2026 version.
2. **Referrals from past clients** — the single highest-conversion source available and almost always under-asked. One sentence: *"who else in your network is trying to figure out AI right now?"*
3. **Warm network** — anyone who has attended a workshop, spoken at the same event, or engaged repeatedly on LinkedIn.
4. **Wednesday workshop attendees** — enterprise buyers self-qualify by showing up. Look at who has been attending.

**What to sell:** a one-day or two-day AI enablement workshop, priced at benchmark, with the 4C audit as the pre-work. Not a proposal for an automation build — training is what enterprise buys from an outsider, and it creates the internal champion who later sponsors the build.

**Why this is also the durable position:** the research is unambiguous that in-housing is the defining agency churn factor of 2026, and that firms who *teach the client's team* retain far better than firms who execute for them. **Training is the moat, not the loss-leader.**

**Cold outbound stays running** at 30 min/day per the existing Lead Engine cadence — but it is not the cash plan for this sprint. It is the pipeline for the next one.

## Track B — Audience harvest

**The asset:** a 46-slide webinar, eight blocks, six poll beats, demo-before-offer, value stack. It has converted cold. It is running occasionally instead of on a schedule, and it terminates at ₹5,000 with nothing above it.

**Three jobs, in order:**

### 1. Build the rungs above ₹5,000 (do this first)

Nothing else in this track matters if buyers still have nowhere to ascend. See [`../00-strategy/offer-ladder.md`](../00-strategy/offer-ladder.md) for the priced ladder. Minimum viable: one mid-tier above the bootcamp and one named high-ticket offer.

### 2. Run the webinar to the existing audience

Promoted properly — not one mention, a real launch sequence across YouTube, the list, WhatsApp, Skool and Community Tab.

### 3. Reactivate every past attendee who didn't buy

Free pipeline that has already raised its hand. Most sequences quit after two or three touches; a reactivation campaign to people who watched a full webinar and didn't purchase is the cheapest revenue in the business.

### The sprint math

Published benchmarks, applied conservatively ([sources](../06-evidence/market-research.md#5-webinar-and-launch-benchmarks)):

| Stage | Benchmark | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Registration → live | median **41.6%** | bottom quartile 28.4% — plan for the bottom quartile |
| Attendee → offer (educational) | 15–25% | |
| Attendee → offer (high-ticket) | **5–12%** | use this for anything above ₹25K |
| Interactive polls | up to **3× CTA clicks** | **already in the deck** |
| Engaged attendees | **30% more likely** to convert | |

Work the funnel backwards from a revenue target rather than forwards from a subscriber count — subscriber-to-registration rates vary far too much to model honestly. Set the target, compute the registrations needed at bottom-quartile show-up and mid-range conversion, then decide whether organic alone gets there or paid promotion is needed.

**One honest caveat:** creator-economy sources reporting ₹50L–2Cr per launch to a 100K audience have every incentive to inflate. Discount hard. Even heavily discounted, one properly-run launch plausibly clears a month or more of current revenue — and the asset is already built.

## Rules

- **Publishing does not pause.** Tue/Fri 9am through all four weeks. The channel is the asset the sprint is harvesting; damaging it to harvest it defeats the purpose.
- **Every price is set in week 1.** Selling against an unset price is the reason the ladder has one rung.
- **Ask for the referral every time.** Every corporate conversation, closed or not.
- **Follow up past three touches.** Most revenue is behind the point where most people stop.
- **No new agents beyond Editor Brief.** The urge to build is what this sprint exists to resist.

---

## Exit criteria

Phase 1 starts when:

1. **One corporate engagement closed or contracted**
2. **One webinar run** to the existing audience, with the reactivation sequence sent
3. **Offer ladder priced and live** — no `[confirm]` anywhere
4. **Tracking working** — every lead traceable to a source
5. **Four lanes running** and the Editor Brief tested

If 1 and 2 both miss, **do not start Phase 1.** Run the sprint again with what week 4's review taught you. A second cash sprint is a far better use of month two than a content build funded by a shrinking runway.

---

## What this sprint is really testing

Beyond the cash: whether the business can sell at a price that matches its proof. If corporate training closes at benchmark, the entire ladder re-anchors upward and Phase 2's installed-OS pricing follows it. If it doesn't close, that is the most important thing to learn in month one rather than month six.
