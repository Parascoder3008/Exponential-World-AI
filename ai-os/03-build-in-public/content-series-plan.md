# Build in Public — The Content Series

**The insight:** building the OS and making content about the OS are the same activity. Not "document the journey" as an afterthought — the build *is* the content, the content *is* the QA, and the finished series *is* the sales asset for the product.

**The unit:** one agent = one long-form + one short + one LinkedIn post + one Skool drop.

Twelve to thirteen agents over thirteen weeks maps almost exactly onto two publishes a week. The content calendar does not need inventing; it already exists as the build order.

---

## Why this works

Three things happen at once that would otherwise cost three separate efforts:

1. **Proof accumulates publicly.** After 90 days there are 26 videos showing a business being built with agents. No portfolio page competes with that.
2. **The audience becomes the beta.** Comments surface the edge cases the team missed. Free QA from people motivated to be right.
3. **The product pre-sells itself.** By the time agent packs exist, the audience has watched each one get built and knows what it does. The pack is not a new offer — it is the thing they have been watching for three months.

The risk it removes is the biggest one in the plan: building in private for 90 days and discovering nobody wanted it.

## The series

Working title: **"Main ek AI company bana raha hoon"** — *I'm building an AI company.* Hinglish, first-person, ongoing.

Positioning within it stays fixed: **a business person building this, not an engineer.** Every episode answers "what did this do for the business", never "how does the model work". The moment it drifts technical it stops serving the audience it was built for.

Each episode follows the same shape:

| Beat | Content |
|---|---|
| **Hook** | The problem this agent solved, stated as a cost — hours, money, or a thing that kept breaking |
| **Before** | How it was done by hand. Honest, including the embarrassing parts |
| **Build** | The agent, on screen. Real output, not a diagram |
| **Result** | What changed. Numbers where they exist, "too early to tell" where they don't |
| **You** | How a viewer does this in their own business this week |
| **Loop** | What's being built next — the open loop into the next episode |

The **Before** beat is the one that carries the series. Showing the mess is what makes it credible, and it is what nobody else does.

## Per-episode derivatives

From one long-form, produced by the Repurpose agent, operator-run:

| Asset | Platform | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| 3–5 shorts | YouTube, IG, FB | The single sharpest moment each |
| Long-form post | LinkedIn | The business lesson, English, for the client audience |
| Carousel | Instagram | The framework as slides, Hinglish hooks |
| Thread | X | The build, step by step |
| Broadcast | WhatsApp | Conversational, one link |
| Post + files | Skool | **The actual agent**, for members |
| Local post | GMB | Short, local |

The Skool drop is the one that converts. Members get the working agent, not a description of it — which is the low-ticket offer justifying itself every single week without a pitch.

## The Wednesday live build

The existing Wednesday workshop becomes the **live build session**: the week's agent, built in front of people.

This is the single highest-value change to the current schedule, because one 90-minute slot now produces four things at once — a delivery obligation met, content captured, an agent built, and enterprise buyers self-qualifying by showing up.

Format: 15 min recap of last week's result · 45 min live build · 20 min questions · 10 min what's next. Recording goes to Skool. Best 10 minutes becomes a video.

---

## Rules

1. **Ship the agent before the video.** Content about an agent that doesn't work yet is a promise, and promises made on camera become open loops in Brand Brain.
2. **Real numbers or no numbers.** "Too early to tell" is more credible than a number that can't be sourced.
3. **Never technical.** Business person building this. The moment an episode is about the tool rather than the outcome, it is off-position.
4. **Failures ship too.** Week 11 is scheduled, but any real failure should displace whatever was planned. They are the highest-trust episodes available.
5. **The 30-day rule holds in public.** Do not sell an agent the audience has watched being built until it has run the business for 30 days. Saying that out loud is itself a selling point.

## What it produces by week 13

- 26 long-form videos, ~80 shorts, ~26 posts per major platform
- 12–13 agents shipped and documented publicly
- A Skool library of working agents that justifies the membership without a pitch
- A 90-day review that is simultaneously the case study, the sales asset, and the product's documentation
- An audience that has watched the product get built and does not need convincing it works

That last item is the whole point. Most products launch to people who have never seen them. This one launches to people who watched every step.
