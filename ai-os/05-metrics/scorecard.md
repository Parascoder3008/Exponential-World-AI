One weekly view. Assembled Monday morning by the Operator, read by Paras, feeds Chief of Staff.

**The rule that keeps it useful:** it must name **one thing to fix**. A report with three priorities has none.

## The five that matter most

Everything else is context.

1. **Revenue and runway.** At 3–6 months, this decides whether the plan continues as written or reverts to a cash sprint. Recalculate runway monthly, not quarterly.
2. **Offers made.** How many times someone was asked for money. At ₹1–5L/month this number is almost certainly single digits, and it is the real revenue ceiling.
3. **Videos published.** Two a week. Fails first and quietest, and everything downstream depends on it.
4. **Leads by source.** Answers *what produced the last five leads*. Without it, effort allocation is guesswork with confidence.
5. **Paras interventions.** The health check on the whole system. Trending down means the OS is working; flat or rising means lanes are unclear or Brand Brain has gaps.

Two of these are unusual and both are deliberate. **Offers made** is an activity metric on a scorecard otherwise full of outcomes — because at this stage the activity is the constraint. **Paras interventions** measures whether the business is becoming less dependent on one person, which is the actual project.

## The Monday read

Five sentences, written by the Operator, read by Paras. Not a dashboard link — the judgment is the deliverable.

```
Published:      [n]/2. [on rhythm / missed, why]
Best performer: [video] — [CTR] / [AVD]. [why it worked]
Pipeline:       [n] leads, mostly from [source]. [n] calls booked.
Money:          [revenue]. [which offer]
FIX THIS WEEK:  [one thing]
```

The last line goes into the Chief of Staff plan. If it doesn't, the loop is open and the scorecard is decoration.

## Lead Engine's own five

Reviewed every 50 attempts, not weekly — smaller samples say nothing.

1. Reply rate **by signal** — which of the six sourcing signals produces conversations
2. Reply rate **by vertical** — where the transferable proof actually transfers
3. **Audit acceptance rate** — % of replies taking the free 4C audit
4. **Audit → call rate** — is the audit persuasive or merely polite
5. **Bounce rate** — over 3% means sourcing has drifted toward guesswork; stop and fix it before sending anything else

Signal #1 matters more than it looks. After 50 attempts it tells you which signal produces buyers rather than names, and that reshapes the entire engine.

## What not to measure

Deliberately excluded, because each one reliably produces the wrong decision at this stage:

- **Follower counts.** Skool active and leads are the real versions.
- **Impressions.** Reach without action.
- **Time saved.** Unmeasurable and self-flattering. Measure output volume instead.
- **Agents built.** Only agents *in use* count. A built-and-unused agent has negative value.
- **Engagement rate in isolation.** High engagement on content that produces no leads is a hobby.
