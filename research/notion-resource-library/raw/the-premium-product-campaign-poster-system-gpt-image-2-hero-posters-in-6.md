## ⚙️ The Hidden Mechanism — Why a Forced Perspective Hero Composition Beats Standard Product Photography
Every standard AI product photography workflow has the same broken loop:
1. Operator generates a "product hero shot" with the product centered + clean background
2. Output reads as "ecommerce product photo" — accurate but flat
3. Operator generates a "lifestyle shot" with model + product side-by-side
4. Output reads as "stock photo" — present but uninspired
5. Neither shot drives the scroll-stop a paid social campaign needs
Real campaign posters use a specific compositional trick: **forced perspective hero**. The product is positioned closer to the camera than the model. The strong perspective angle makes the product appear monumental — powerful, premium, hero-like.
The forced perspective composition does 3 things at once:
→ **Visual hierarchy locks** — product reads as the dominant element, model reads as the validating element<br>→ **Tactile intimacy** — product feels close enough to touch (which signals desire)<br>→ **Premium positioning** — the perspective angle is the visual language of luxury campaigns
Plus the feature bullet bar at the bottom (icon + label format) adds the ecommerce conversion signal that paid social posters need. Customer sees the hero composition first, the features second, the brand third. Standard scroll-stop architecture.
The fix is not better product photography. The fix is **encoding the forced perspective hero + feature bullet bar into a single prompt** — the thing that turns "AI product photo" into "premium campaign poster."

## ⚙️ The Master Premium Poster GPT Image 2 Prompt
Paste this into GPT Image 2 (ChatGPT, Higgsfield, or Lovart). Swap the 8 brackets.
```javascript
Create a photorealistic premium product
campaign poster in a 4:5 vertical aspect
ratio.

Product: [PRODUCT NAME + KEY VISUAL DETAILS]
Brand style: [PICK 1 — Premium / Luxury /
Tech / Beauty / Food / Drink]
Brand palette: [PRIMARY COLOR + 2 ACCENT
COLORS]

Main subject:
A confident [MODEL ARCHETYPE — gender, age,
appearance] holding or presenting the
[PRODUCT] close to the camera, with the
product in sharp focus and the model slightly
behind it. Use a strong perspective angle so
the product feels powerful, premium, and
hero-like.

Model identity (lock across all generations
of this campaign):
- [AGE RANGE]
- [HAIR DESCRIPTION — color, length, texture]
- [SKIN TONE]
- [BODY/BUILD]
- [WARDROBE — color, fit, style matching brand]
- [EXPRESSION — confident smile / focused /
  warm gaze]

Composition:
- 4:5 vertical orientation
- Product positioned in front of model, in
  sharp focus (product is the hero)
- Model slightly behind product, in soft focus
  or shallow DOF
- Forced perspective: product appears 1.3-1.5x
  larger than realistic scale (powerful,
  monumental)
- Off-axis framing — subject 1/3 right or
  centered with model offset

Brand palette treatment:
- Primary brand color dominates background OR
  model wardrobe
- Background gradient or solid in brand
  palette
- Soft shadow or light bokeh with brand-aligned
  tones

Typography:
- Brand name in top-left or top-right corner
  (clean sans-serif)
- Tagline in [LOCATION — top-center /
  bottom-right]: "[TAGLINE COPY]"
- Brand name + tagline use the brand's
  typography style

Feature bullet bar (bottom of composition):
- 4 horizontal cards at the bottom
- Each card contains: 1 minimal icon + 1 short
  label (2-3 words)
- Features for this product:
  → [FEATURE 1 — e.g. "Real Fruit Taste"]
  → [FEATURE 2 — e.g. "Cold Refreshment"]
  → [FEATURE 3 — e.g. "Vitamin Boost"]
  → [FEATURE 4 — e.g. "Premium Bottle Design"]
- Cards use a clean white or brand-aligned
  background with subtle shadow
- Icons are minimal line-art or simple
  pictograms matching the brand aesthetic

Lighting:
- Soft natural daylight or studio lighting
- High dynamic range, premium color grading
- Subtle gradient highlights on the product
- Skin texture realistic with visible pores
  (no AI smoothing)

Quality anchors:
- Photorealistic (not illustrated, not CGI)
- Sharp focus on product
- 4K resolution
- Premium campaign poster aesthetic
- Magazine-quality composition

Negative prompt:
- No watermark
- No text errors in typography
- No distorted hands or face
- No flat lighting
- No oversaturation
- No cartoon or CGI look
- No model identity drift from spec
- No product distortion
```

## ⚙️ The Brand Palette Lock Mechanic
Brand palette must hold across 4 elements in the poster:
→ Model wardrobe (1-2 brand colors)<br>→ Background (primary brand color or gradient)<br>→ Product (its natural color)<br>→ Typography (brand color + white or black)
The trick: lock the palette in the prompt by specifying exact HEX codes for the 3 brand colors:
```javascript
Brand palette (lock these across composition):
Primary: [#HEX — e.g. #FF7A1A — vibrant orange]
Secondary: [#HEX — e.g. #FFD89B — soft cream]
Accent: [#HEX — e.g. #1A1A1A — deep charcoal
for typography]

Apply the palette across:
- Background dominant color: primary
- Model wardrobe accent: primary OR secondary
- Typography: accent
- Feature bullet cards: white background with
  accent typography + brand-color icons
```
GPT Image 2 doesn't always render HEX codes literally, but specifying the colors with named descriptors anchors the model to the intended palette. Brand consistency holds across 80-90% of generations.

## ⚙️ The Figma Post-Processing Workflow
After the poster generates, polish in Figma + Photoshop.
### Figma — Multi-Format Extraction (3-5 minutes)
1. Import the 4:5 poster PNG
2. Use the slice tool to extract crops:
	- 4:5 vertical (1080×1350) — native
	- 1:1 square (1080×1080) — center crop
	- 9:16 vertical (1080×1920) — extended canvas with brand color fill
	- 16:9 wide (1920×1080) — landscape crop (extend or fill)
3. Export each as 4K PNG
### Photoshop — Brand Typography Overlay (3-5 minutes)
1. Open the poster in Photoshop
2. Add brand logo overlay (top-left or bottom-right corner)
3. Refine typography if GPT Image 2's text rendering needs cleanup
4. Apply subtle color grade adjustment if needed
5. Export final files
### Optional — Animated Hero in Seedance 2.0
Drop the poster into Seedance 2.0 with the optional video extension prompt (Section 6).
Total post-processing: 8-15 minutes per poster across all crops.

## ⚙️ What NOT to Do
→ Don't generate posters without the forced perspective specification. Without it, you get standard "model + product" composition which reads as stock photo.
→ Don't skip the feature bullet bar. It's the difference between "hero image" and "campaign poster."
→ Don't use more than 4 features. 5+ reads cluttered. 3 reads thin. 4 is the calibrated sweet spot.
→ Don't pick contradictory brand styles for a single brand. A skincare brand using Tech style creates aesthetic confusion. Match style to brand positioning.
→ Don't rotate model archetypes within the same campaign. Audience pattern recognition needs consistency. Stick with 1-2 archetypes per quarter.
→ Don't skip the HEX color anchoring. Naming colors with descriptors anchors palette better than letting the model freelance.
→ Don't deploy without the 5-point QC. Forced perspective fails \\~15% of the time on first generation. Always verify.
→ Don't generate posters at low resolution. 4K minimum (1080×1350 at native 4:5). Below that and crop quality degrades.
→ Don't use this system for editorial brand pillar campaigns. Real shoots still win for hero brand pieces. Use AI posters for paid social volume production.
→ Don't replace the 6-angle ecommerce composite with this. Different use case — 6-angle for product detail page multi-angle, posters for campaign hero. Run both.
→ Don't write generic feature labels. "Premium Quality" reads vague. "Cold-Pressed Daily" reads specific. Match labels to the product's actual differentiators.

## ⚙️ Troubleshooting
<table header-row="true">
<tr>
<td>Problem</td>
<td>Fix</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Forced perspective fails (product not closer to camera than model)</td>
<td>Reinforce "product clearly closer to camera, 1.3-1.5x larger than realistic, model in soft focus behind product" explicitly.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Brand palette drifts</td>
<td>Add HEX codes + descriptive color names + repeat the palette anchor 2x in the prompt.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Feature bullet bar text garbled</td>
<td>GPT Image 2 mangles typography. Render the bar in Figma post-extraction if labels won't render cleanly.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Model identity drifts between generations</td>
<td>Save the model archetype as a saved prompt block. Re-use verbatim across every poster generation.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Product label distorts</td>
<td>Product description not specific enough. Include exact label color + position + proportions.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Lighting feels flat</td>
<td>Reinforce "premium campaign poster lighting, high dynamic range, dramatic studio with subtle gradient highlights."</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Composition feels like stock photo</td>
<td>Forced perspective specification too weak. Strengthen: "product hero positioned aggressively forward, model serves as supporting validating element only."</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Feature icons don't render correctly</td>
<td>GPT Image 2 struggles with small icon detail. Render simplified line-art icons in Figma post-extraction.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Background gradient too aggressive</td>
<td>Specify "subtle gradient" instead of "vibrant gradient." Lower color saturation in the prompt.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Wardrobe drifts from brand palette</td>
<td>Reinforce "wardrobe MUST be brand-aligned palette" with explicit color reference.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Costs add up faster than expected</td>
<td>Track regenerations per poster. Aim for 1.5 generations average (1 success, 0.5 regenerates). Above 2 = prompt needs refinement.</td>
</tr>
</table>
