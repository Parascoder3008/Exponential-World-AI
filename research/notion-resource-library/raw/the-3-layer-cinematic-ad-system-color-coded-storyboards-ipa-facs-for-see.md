## ⚙️ The Hidden Mechanism — Why Color-Coding Fixes Multi-Instruction Confusion
Every standard AI video prompt has the same broken loop:
1. Operator writes a plain-English prompt mixing body movement + camera direction + lighting + framing + emotion
2. GPT Image 2 (or Seedance) parses the prompt and picks one or two instructions to honor
3. The rest get ignored or, worse, drawn INTO the scene as if they were objects ("trembling hands" becomes a literal hand drawing instead of a motion direction)
4. Output looks amateur because instructions and scene content got tangled
The model isn't bad at any single instruction. It's bad at **separating** instruction layers when they all live in the same prompt block.
The color-coded annotation fix is structural: instead of putting all instructions in one prompt, you create a storyboard image where each instruction type uses a different color. Red arrows = body movement. Blue arrows = camera. Green marks = framing. Orange = lighting. Purple = vocal emphasis. Black = lens notes.
The model now treats colored annotations as **metadata** rather than scene content. Body movement arrows don't get drawn AS body parts. Camera direction doesn't get rendered AS a camera object in the scene. Each color tells the model "this is direction, not content."
Then 2 additional precision layers go in the Seedance prompt:
→ **IPA** (International Phonetic Alphabet): instead of writing "she says 'I move through silence,'" you write `/aɪ muːv θruː ˈsaɪləns/`. Seedance now renders precise phoneme-by-phoneme mouth shapes instead of guessing.
→ **FACS** (Facial Action Coding System): instead of writing "she looks exhausted but determined," you write `AU1+AU4 tension, AU5 intensity, AU43 exhausted blink`. Seedance now renders the specific micro-expressions instead of defaulting to generic AI faces.
The fix is not better prompts. The fix is **separating instruction layers by encoding system** — colors for visual direction, IPA for phonemes, FACS for micro-expressions.

## ⚙️ Step 1 — Define the 12-Beat Narrative Arc
Before any generation, lock the 12-beat arc. Each beat = 1 storyboard panel = roughly 1.25 seconds of final video.
The 12-beat default structure:
1. Establishing shot (wide setting, character introduction)
2. Setup beat (initial pose, mood establishment)
3. Conflict beat (movement begins, tension introduced)
4. First peak (sharp movement or emotional reveal)
5. Tracking beat (follow-shot energy, momentum builds)
6. Overhead or wide beat (visual variety, scale)
7. Close-up beat (emotional intimacy)
8. Detail beat (hands, product, key feature)
9. Dynamic angle beat (Dutch angle, low angle, orbit)
10. Compressed long-lens beat (subject isolated)
11. Orbit or move beat (camera circles subject)
12. Final hero beat (held pose under spotlight, brand moment)
Adapt the arc by vertical (see 6 templates below). The 12-beat structure is the spine.

## ⚙️ Step 5 — Animate in Seedance 2.0 with 12-to-15 Compression
Paste the master Seedance prompt (Section 12) into Seedance 2.0. The prompt references the color-coded storyboard, includes the IPA script, and includes the FACS emotion direction per beat.
Seedance compresses the 12-beat sequence into 15 seconds total. Each beat appears as a fast motion snapshot (not a full-length action). Use urgent rhythm, quick cuts, match cuts, and whip transitions between beats. No pauses until the final beat.
Final beat holds for the last 1 second under one harsh isolated spotlight — this is the brand moment, the visual anchor.
Generation time: 4-6 minutes for the full 15-second cinematic.

## ⚙️ The 12-Panel Compression Logic
Why 12 panels and 15 seconds specifically:
→ 12 panels × \\~1.25 seconds each = 15 seconds total<br>→ Each panel becomes a fast motion snapshot, not a full action<br>→ Quick cuts + match cuts + whip transitions hide the snapshot nature, build cinematic urgency<br>→ Final beat holds for 1 second under spotlight = the brand anchor moment
You CAN extend to 18 panels for 22-second ads or compress to 9 panels for 11-second hooks. But 12-to-15 is the calibrated sweet spot for ad platforms that prioritize short-form attention windows.
The compression mechanism is what makes the format ad-platform-ready while preserving cinematic narrative depth.

## ⚙️ What NOT to Do
→ Don't write multi-instruction prompts in plain English. Mixing body movement + camera + lighting + emotion in one block guarantees the model picks one and ignores the rest. Use the color-coded layer system.
→ Don't skip the IPA layer when lipsync matters. English spelling produces approximated mouth shapes. IPA produces phoneme-perfect ones.
→ Don't stack too many FACS codes per beat. 2-3 codes per beat is the right density. 5+ produces an over-acted, distorted expression.
→ Don't use the wrong color for the wrong instruction type. Red for body, blue for camera. Mixing them re-introduces the confusion the system fixes.
→ Don't generate without a character reference. The system locks character identity via the reference. Without it, identity drifts across the 12 panels.
→ Don't render storyboards in full color. The pencil-line black-and-white aesthetic is part of the mechanism — it tells the model "this is storyboard, not final scene."
→ Don't compress past 9 panels or extend past 18 without recalibrating. The 12-to-15 ratio is calibrated. Other ratios need adjustment to the beat rhythm.
→ Don't skip the final hero beat. Panel 12 + the 1-second hold under spotlight is the brand anchor. Without it, the ad has nowhere to land.
→ Don't run the system on dialog-heavy scripts. IPA + FACS is calibrated for 1-3 short lines. Long dialog overwhelms the lipsync precision.
→ Don't expect every FACS code to render perfectly the first time. Some Action Units render more clearly than others. Test, learn which codes your specific use case responds to.
→ Don't ignore the annotation legend in the storyboard output. The model adds it automatically — keep it visible during QC, hide it before final deploy if needed.

## ⚙️ Troubleshooting
<table header-row="true">
<tr>
<td>Problem</td>
<td>Fix</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Storyboard annotations look like scene objects</td>
<td>The model treated colors as content. Re-emphasize "annotation color system" in the prompt and add "annotations are directional, not drawn into scenes."</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Lipsync still robotic with IPA</td>
<td>The IPA may be incorrect. Verify via [ipa-reader.com](http://ipa-reader.com/). Some words have multiple valid pronunciations — pick the one matching your accent.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>FACS codes produce no visible difference</td>
<td>Action Units have varying render strength. Start with high-impact codes (AU25, AU26, AU43, AU12, AU5). Lower-impact codes (AU14, AU17) are subtler.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Character identity drifts across 12 panels</td>
<td>Increase character reference fidelity. Lock identity strength to 0.9+. Re-upload reference at higher resolution.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>12 panels render at inconsistent quality</td>
<td>The model fatigued mid-generation. Reduce to 9 panels OR regenerate with shorter beat descriptions.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Camera direction annotations get drawn AS cameras</td>
<td>Strengthen "blue arrows = camera MOVEMENT, not camera OBJECT" in the prompt.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Final hero beat doesn't feel anchored</td>
<td>The spotlight + 1-second hold is calibrated. Reinforce "Beat 12: 1-second hold, harsh isolated spotlight, brand reveal moment" in both prompts.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Whip transitions feel jarring</td>
<td>Reduce transition speed in the Seedance prompt — change "whip transitions" to "smooth match cuts with slight motion blur."</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Output too long (over 15s)</td>
<td>Reduce the beat count to 10-11 panels OR increase compression urgency in the Seedance prompt.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Output too short (under 15s)</td>
<td>Add 1-2 panels OR increase beat hold duration on the calmer panels (panels 1, 5, 8).</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Character lipsync visible but wrong word</td>
<td>The IPA phoneme is off. Re-verify the IPA transcription. The model is faithful to the phoneme it sees.</td>
</tr>
</table>
