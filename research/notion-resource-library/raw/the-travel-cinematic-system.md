## ⚙️ The Hidden Mechanism — Why STRICT Face-Lock Beats Standard Character Consistency
Every standard AI travel film has the same failure mode:
1. Operator prompts "same character throughout film"
2. Seedance interprets loosely
3. Character face drifts by scene 2 or 3
4. Wardrobe changes subtly between scenes
5. Viewer clocks the identity drift within seconds
The fix is structural: STRICT FACE & IDENTITY LOCK language + face-only reference + explicit wardrobe descriptor + Opus 5's context bandwidth holding all scene prompts in one direction. When Seedance receives explicit LOCK language + high-strength identity references, it treats the character as a fixed anchor across every scene render.
Same for wardrobe. "Same outfit" fails. "Oversized black leather jacket with relaxed silhouette and subtle vintage texture, crisp white button-down shirt underneath slightly untucked, loose-fit baggy blue denim jeans with modern streetwear cut, clean white sneakers" holds across every scene.
The visual language of premium travel creators (Aime Leon Dore, La Ligne, editorial travel accounts) inherits authority when identity + wardrobe stay pristine across urban scene changes. Viewers subconsciously code it as high-production.
The fix is not "better character consistency." The fix is **STRICT FACE & IDENTITY LOCK language + full wardrobe descriptor enforced across every scene** — the thing that turns AI travel films from obvious-AI to indistinguishable-from-editorial-shoots.

## ⚙️ Step 2 — The Character + Wardrobe STRICT Lock Prompt
Character identity is the entire mechanic. Get this wrong and every downstream scene fails.
### The Character Sheet (Build in GPT Image 2)
Generate a character sheet with 4 sections:
**Section 1**: Face close-up (3/4 view, warm neutral background, matching lighting to intended city scenes)
**Section 2**: Full body (front + side + back turnaround, in exact outfit)
**Section 3**: Outfit detail (jacket close-up + shirt close-up + shoes close-up + accessories close-up)
**Section 4**: Character metadata (name, age, height, personality descriptors)
### The Outfit Descriptor Formula
Every outfit needs 5-8 specific elements. Vague outfit descriptors cause drift.
**Vague (fails)**:
```javascript
"Oversized black jacket and jeans"
```
**Production-grade (holds)**:
```javascript
"Oversized black leather jacket with relaxed
silhouette and subtle vintage texture. Crisp
white button-down shirt underneath, slightly
untucked for a casual luxury look. Loose-fit
baggy blue denim jeans with a modern streetwear
cut. Clean white sneakers. Minimal accessories:
simple silver chain, black watch, effortless
hairstyle."
```
### The STRICT FACE & IDENTITY LOCK Language
Add this exact phrasing to every scene prompt:
```javascript
STRICT FACE & IDENTITY LOCK — maintain the
exact same facial features in every scene.
Character face reference attached as
@character_face at 0.97 lock strength. Outfit
identical to reference sheet, no wardrobe
changes, no accessory drift.
```
This is what makes the difference between "different-looking guy in each scene" and "same character across the whole film."
### The Face-Only Reference
For maximum identity accuracy, additionally attach a face-only reference (cropped from the full character sheet):
```javascript
Face-only reference: @character_face_only.jpg
attached separately. Use as face identity
anchor at 0.98 lock strength on all scenes
featuring the character.
```
Kills mid-film face drift.

## ⚙️ Step 3 — The 4-Scene 15-Second Travel Arc Structure
The narrative structure. Every brand travel film uses this exact 4-beat arc.
### Scene 1 (0-3s) — Arrival in [City]
**Purpose**: Establishing shot + character entering city context.
**Camera language**:
- Aerial drone establishing shot of city skyline
- Dive between skyscrapers OR sweep across neon-lit streets
- Transition into street level following character
**Action**:
- Character exits train station / airport / hotel
- First street-level moment (walking into crowd)
- City lights + neon signs reflect on wet pavement
**Audio**:
- Train announcements + footsteps + city chatter
- Distant street music
- Ambient city hum
**Voice-Over**:
- Opening reflective line
- 6-10 words
- Sets the "why I came here" premise
**Example voice-over**: "I came here looking for a new experience..."
### Scene 2 (3-7s) — [City] Street Moments
**Purpose**: Fast-paced montage through iconic city locations.
**Camera language**:
- Fast-paced cinematic montage with smooth transitions
- Fashion film shots mixed with travel documentary shots
- Slow motion, shallow depth of field, cinematic close-ups
**Action**:
- Character walks through iconic city moments (Shibuya crossing / Times Square / Paris cafes / LA sunset boulevard)
- Close-ups of jacket / sneakers / coffee cup / product in hand
- Sitting inside cafe or ramen shop, city lights through window
- Walking through narrow alley with atmospheric lighting
**Audio**:
- City atmosphere fades in and out
- Music builds subtly
**Voice-Over**:
- Bridge line
- 6-10 words
- Sets up the "but this city had a story" turn
**Example voice-over**: "But Tokyo had a story waiting for me..."
### Scene 3 (7-11s) — Rooftop [City] Moment
**Purpose**: Elevated aspirational beat + character contemplation.
**Camera language**:
- Smooth handheld tracking shot moving behind character
- Slow push-in close-up capturing expression at end
**Action**:
- Character walks onto rooftop
- Wardrobe catches wind (jacket flowing / hair moving)
- City glows beneath character
- Fireworks / sunset / stars in the distance
**Audio**:
- Music rises to peak
- City sounds fade to background
**Voice-Over**:
- Silent moment (contemplative)
- OR very brief impactful line
### Scene 4 (11-15s) — Sunrise / Sunset Ending
**Purpose**: Final aspirational payoff + brand thesis close.
**Camera language**:
- Wide cinematic shot from behind character
- Final close-up of confident expression
**Action**:
- Character stands above city at magic hour
- Hands in jacket pockets, looking over waking / sleeping city
- Golden light reflects on wardrobe
- Skyline fades into morning / evening haze
**Audio**:
- Music peaks then fades
- Voice-over lands over final shot
**Voice-Over**:
- Closing thesis line
- 8-12 words
- Ties the brand's philosophical position
**Example voice-over**: "Sometimes, you don't find a place... a place finds you."
**End Text**: "24 Hours in [CITY]" + brand tagline
**Caption for post**: "Lost in [CITY], found in the moment. 24 hours. Endless streets. One unforgettable story."

## ⚙️ Step 5 — The Seedance 2.0 4K HDR Rendering Protocol
Take Opus 5's 4-scene brief and render on Seedance 2.0.
### Render Setup
1. Open Higgsfield or Replicate → select Seedance 2.0 Pro
2. Attach character reference + face-only reference
3. Paste full 4-scene brief + STYLE + CHARACTER + OUTFIT blocks
4. Set output: 9:16 (Instagram Reels default) OR 16:9 (YouTube pillar), 15 seconds, 4K HDR
5. Enable native audio (optional — most travel films use added music + voice-over)
6. Render
Render time: 8-15 minutes for 15-second continuous film.
Cost: $7-$10 per generation.
### QC Pass
After render, verify:
→ Character face consistent across all 4 scenes<br>→ Wardrobe unchanged (no jacket drift, no accessory changes)<br>→ City aesthetic reads as intended destination<br>→ Camera work reads as cinematic (not stuttery)<br>→ Audio arc coherent
If any check fails, re-render with tightened STRICT LOCK language.

## ⚙️ Step 6 — The CapCut Assembly Protocol
Voice-over + music + polish. 5-8 minutes.
### 5-Minute Assembly
**Minutes 0-1**: Import<br>→ Import Seedance MP4<br>→ Verify 15-second duration + aspect ratio
**Minutes 1-3**: Voice-Over Layer<br>→ Generate voice-over lines (ElevenLabs or authentic recording)<br>→ Trim silence, measure duration to match scene timing<br>→ Mux over silent video<br>→ Volume: voice-over at -6 to -9 dB, dominant layer
**Minutes 3-4**: Music<br>→ Add cinematic travel instrumental (city-appropriate genre)<br>→ Duck volume during voice-over moments<br>→ Boost during transition beats<br>→ Volume: -12 to -14 dB (background)
**Minutes 4-5**: City Ambient<br>→ Add subtle city ambient audio (train / crowd / traffic)<br>→ Volume: -18 to -14 dB (very subtle)
**Minutes 5-6**: Text Overlays<br>→ End text overlay ("24 Hours in [CITY]" + brand tagline)<br>→ Font: elegant serif or bold sans-serif matching brand<br>→ Fade in at Scene 4 end frame
**Minutes 6-7**: Export<br>→ 4K resolution, 30fps<br>→ File naming: `[BRAND]_TRAVEL-FILM_[CITY]_v1.mp4`
Total polish time: 5-8 minutes.

## ⚙️ What NOT to Do
→ Don't skip STRICT FACE & IDENTITY LOCK language. It's the entire mechanic.
→ Don't use vague outfit descriptors. 5-8 specific elements minimum per outfit.
→ Don't skip the face-only reference anchor. Character sheets with multiple faces cause drift.
→ Don't change wardrobe between scenes. Continuity is what makes travel films read as editorial-quality.
→ Don't skip voice-over. Travel cinematics without voice-over feel like generic city footage.
→ Don't use over-produced voice-over. Warm authentic tone matches editorial travel aesthetic.
→ Don't measure travel films on CTR. Measure view-through + saves + brand recall + follower lift.
→ Don't deploy on TikTok without slightly handheld realism. Add subtle camera character in prompt.
→ Don't skip the city aesthetic library. Wrong city visuals = tourists-not-locals feel.
→ Don't use the same voice-over line archetype across every film. Rotate archetypes per brand context.
→ Don't skip the End Text + Caption. They're what tie the film to the brand narrative.
→ Don't ship without music + city ambient audio. Silent travel films feel unfinished.
