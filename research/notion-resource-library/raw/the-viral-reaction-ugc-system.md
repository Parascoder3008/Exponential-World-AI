## ⚙️ The Hidden Mechanism — Why Reaction Format Beats Talking-Head UGC
Every standard UGC ad workflow has the same broken loop:
1. Brief creator with product pitch
2. Creator films talking-head pitch with product in frame
3. Creator submits 30-60-second flat pitch
4. Operator deploys, watches scroll-past rates collapse
5. Repeat with different creators, same result
The fix is structural: reaction format reverses the order. Hook with a reaction to a problem FIRST, reveal the product as the solution SECOND. The viewer's ad-detector reads "organic content" for the first 3-5 seconds because there's no product in frame yet. That 3-5 seconds is the entire battle.
The viral pattern that drove 8M+ views on the original creator's format is the same pattern that works for DTC:
→ Front-cam reaction hooks (face + emotion + relatable problem) bypass the ad-detector<br>→ Product cut-in lands AFTER the viewer is already invested in the reaction<br>→ Resolution beat ties the product to the emotional payoff
Talking-head UGC fails at the first frame because the product is already in view + the creator is already in pitch mode. Reaction UGC succeeds because the first frame looks like organic content.
The fix is not better individual creators. The fix is **the reaction beat structure** — the thing that gets the viewer past the first 3 seconds before they realize they're watching an ad.

## ⚙️ The 7 Reaction Beat Structures
Each beat structure is a hook archetype. Pick one per ad based on your product + audience.
### Beat 1 — Problem-Shock
**Hook**: Avatar reacts to a problem they just discovered.
**Example opener**: "Wait, you guys are paying [$X] for [category solution]? I just found [product] for [$Y]."
**Best for**: Pricing-disruption brands, hidden-cost reveal categories, alternatives to overpriced incumbents.
### Beat 2 — Before-After
**Hook**: Avatar shows a problem state, cuts to result state, then explains what bridged the two.
**Example opener**: "I had [problem condition] for [time period]. This is what fixed it in [time]."
**Best for**: Skincare, supplements, fitness, anything with visual transformation.
### Beat 3 — "I-Tried-Everything"
**Hook**: Avatar lists what didn't work, then reveals what did.
**Example opener**: "I tried [Option A], [Option B], [Option C]. Nothing worked. Then I tried [product]."
**Best for**: Categories with high failed-purchase rates (hair growth, sleep, weight loss, productivity tools).
### Beat 4 — "Wait-What"
**Hook**: Avatar reacts to a counter-intuitive fact about the product or category.
**Example opener**: "Wait, [counter-intuitive product mechanic]? How is this even legal?"
**Best for**: Products with surprising mechanisms (cold-press tech, fermentation, AI features, unusual ingredients).
### Beat 5 — POV Reaction
**Hook**: Avatar is in a real-life moment + product appears as natural part of the scene.
**Example opener**: "[Scene-setting line: e.g. 'POV: you just got your [product] and it's a Tuesday']."
**Best for**: Lifestyle brands, fashion, food/bev, anything tied to a specific moment or occasion.
### Beat 6 — Friend-Recommendation
**Hook**: Avatar shares a recommendation framed as a friend tip, not a pitch.
**Example opener**: "Okay if you're [target demographic descriptor], you need to hear this."
**Best for**: Niche-targeted products, community-tagged brands, recommendation-driven categories.
### Beat 7 — Founder-Discovery
**Hook**: Avatar reacts to discovering a founder's backstory or why the product exists.
**Example opener**: "I just found out why [founder] built [product] and now I understand everything."
**Best for**: Brands with strong founder narrative, mission-driven brands, premium positioning brands.

## ⚙️ The 3-Act Reaction Structure
Every reaction UGC ad follows this 3-act structure regardless of beat type.
### Act 1 — Hook Reaction (0-5 seconds)
→ Front-cam avatar shot, face centered<br>→ Reaction emotion lands in first 2 seconds<br>→ NO product in frame<br>→ Pattern interrupt opening line (from chosen beat structure)<br>→ Hook ends with implicit promise of payoff
### Act 2 — Problem Expansion (5-15 seconds)
→ Avatar elaborates the problem or context<br>→ Builds tension toward the resolution<br>→ Product can be teased (mentioned by name) but not yet shown demo<br>→ Specific detail that confirms avatar's credibility on the topic<br>→ Sets up the bridge to product demo
### Act 3 — Product Demo + Resolution (15-30 seconds)
→ Cut to product demo (screen recording, product close-up, in-use shot)<br>→ Show the specific mechanic that solves the problem<br>→ Return to avatar for reaction to result<br>→ Resolution beat (satisfaction, relief, surprise, confidence)<br>→ Soft CTA (URL in bio, comment for code, etc.)
Total length: 25-35 seconds optimal. 60 seconds maximum for higher-consideration products.

## ⚙️ Step 2 — The Claude → Arcads Production Chain
Take the script from Step 1, render the avatar performance in Arcads.
### Arcads Setup (One-Time)
→ Sign up for Arcads (\\~$110/month for unlimited generations)<br>→ Build avatar roster (5-10 archetypes per brand vertical — see 6 templates below)<br>→ Upload brand voice samples if using custom voice clones<br>→ Set default aspect ratio: 9:16 vertical<br>→ Set default length tolerance: 25-35 seconds
### Per-Ad Rendering Flow
1. Open Arcads, pick avatar archetype matching script's voice tone
2. Paste the Act 1 + Act 2 + Act 3 dialogue into the script field
3. Pick voice clone (matched to avatar)
4. Add performance direction notes (emotion arc per act)
5. Render Act 1 + Act 2 as continuous front-cam shot
6. Render Act 3 resolution beat as continuous front-cam shot (separate render)
7. Pick up both Arcads outputs
Render time per ad: 5-8 minutes total (2 renders + queue time).
### Product Demo Capture (Parallel Task)
While Arcads renders, capture the product demo segment:
→ Screen recording: use QuickTime or Loom for any SaaS / app / digital product<br>→ Product close-up: 4K phone capture of product in real-use scenario<br>→ In-use shot: 4K phone capture of product being used by hand (no full human in frame — keeps continuity with avatar)<br>→ Length: 5-10 seconds raw, will be trimmed in CapCut

## ⚙️ Step 3 — The 2-Minute CapCut Polish (iPhone Front-Cam Aesthetic)
This is the step that kills the AI tell. Skip this and your reaction UGC reads as obviously AI within 2 seconds.
### CapCut Polish Checklist
→ Import Act 1+2 avatar render<br>→ Import Act 3 avatar render<br>→ Import product demo capture<br>→ Trim Act 1+2 to 12-15 seconds, end on emotional peak<br>→ Hard cut to product demo (no transition — feels native)<br>→ Trim product demo to 8-12 seconds, end on the "solved" moment<br>→ Hard cut to Act 3 avatar resolution shot (3-5 seconds)<br>→ Apply subtle CapCut filter: "Original" or "Vivid HDR" (NOT "Beauty" filters)<br>→ Add slight grain: CapCut → Effects → Grain → 15-25% intensity<br>→ Add slight handshake: CapCut → Effects → Handheld → 8-12% intensity (front-cam only)<br>→ Caption: bold sans-serif, white with black stroke, bottom-third placement<br>→ Caption font: TikTok Sans or Inter Bold (NOT system default)<br>→ Export: 1080×1920 vertical, 30fps, original quality

## ⚙️ What NOT to Do
→ Don't skip the CapCut polish. Raw Arcads output reads as AI within 2 seconds. The polish is what kills the AI tell.
→ Don't show the product in Act 1. The entire mechanism is hiding the product until the viewer is invested in the reaction. Product in frame = ad-detector triggered = scroll past.
→ Don't pitch in Act 1. The avatar is reacting, not selling. Sales language in the opening = trust killer.
→ Don't use system fonts for captions. Bold sans-serif with stroke matches TikTok native styling. System fonts read as generic ad.
→ Don't use transitions between cuts. Hard cuts replicate native phone editing. Transitions read as paid ad production.
→ Don't ignore the avatar archetype match. Skincare reactions need a skincare-credible avatar (woman 25-35, bathroom morning). Wrong archetype = lost credibility.
→ Don't render avatars perfectly still. Add slight handshake in CapCut. Phones held by humans tremor slightly. Perfect stability = AI tell.
→ Don't write reaction scripts longer than 35 seconds total. Optimal is 25-30 seconds. Longer kills hold rate.
→ Don't deploy without testing all 7 beat structures over the first month. Each product has a winning beat structure. You can't know which without testing.
→ Don't bury the CTA in the avatar's dialogue. End with soft CTA only ("link in bio", "code in bio"). Hard CTA in dialogue reads as ad.
→ Don't recycle the same beat structure if it stops scaling. Audience pattern recognition kills repeat beat structures within 2-3 weeks of high spend.
