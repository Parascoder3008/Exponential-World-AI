## Workflow 1 — UGC Ads Workflow
**Time:** Half a day of manual work compressed into 5 minutes.
You drop in 1 product photo, pick the format, and the workflow generates a character, places your product in their hand, matches the right voice to the persona, handles the motion, and delivers a finished UGC ad.
### What you input:
→ 1 clean product photo (white background, 1024x1024 PNG)<br>→ Format choice (9:16, 1:1, 16:9)<br>→ Tone direction (casual UGC, founder POV, professional reviewer)<br>→ ICP description (1 line)
### What you get back:
→ Generated AI character with locked identity<br>→ Product placed naturally in scene<br>→ Voice matched to persona demographic<br>→ Natural motion and lip-sync<br>→ Finished video ready to upload
### Pro tips:
→ Use the same character across 20+ variations to build audience recognition<br>→ Generate the first ad. Verify the persona looks right. Then run 19 more variations from the same locked persona.<br>→ Always specify ICP. "Women 28-45, busy moms, premium-curious" beats blank.

## Workflow 2 — Cinematic Scene Workflow
**Time:** 6 minutes from 1 image to a 9-scene cinematic video.
You feed in 1 image and the workflow produces a full cinematic sequence built around that starting frame. Proper scene cuts, camera motion, visual continuity already handled.
### What you input:
→ 1 high-quality starting image (the hero frame of your ad)<br>→ Scene direction (action, emotion, environment progression)
### What you get back:
→ 9-scene cinematic video with shot variety<br>→ Camera movement decisions (push-in, orbit, rack focus, handheld)<br>→ Visual continuity across all scenes<br>→ Pacing and transitions tuned for cinematic feel
### When to use:
→ Brand spots and hero ads<br>→ Product launch teasers<br>→ YouTube pre-roll where you have 30 seconds to tell a story<br>→ Pinterest video pins
### Example image-to-output prompt:
```javascript
Starting frame: [DESCRIBE YOUR HERO IMAGE — subject,
location, lighting, mood]

Direction: cinematic 9-scene sequence showing
[NARRATIVE PROGRESSION]. Use shot variety —
establishing wide, medium tracking, close-up insert,
hero pull-out. Maintain golden hour lighting and
shallow depth of field across every scene.

Pacing: gentle, music-led, no rapid cuts.
Audio: cinematic underscore, brand sting at end.
```

## Workflow 3 — Time-Freeze Effect Workflow
**Time:** 4 minutes vs. 3 days of manual work.
This is the cinematic time-freeze shot you've seen hit millions of views on Reels. Motion stops mid-scene. Camera orbits around a frozen moment.
### Manual approach (don't do this):
You'd need a starting image, motion reference video, freeze frame selection, orbital camera motion pass, re-animation, final editing. 3 days minimum if you know every step.
### Workflow approach:
You give it a starting image and an action description. Done in 4 minutes.
### What you input:
→ 1 starting image of your subject in motion<br>→ Description of the moment you want frozen<br>→ Orbital direction (left-to-right, full 360, half arc)
### What you get back:
→ Cinematic clip with motion that freezes mid-scene<br>→ Camera orbit around the frozen subject<br>→ Re-animation that resumes naturally after the freeze
### Best use cases:
→ Product launch reveals (freeze on the unboxing moment)<br>→ Athletic brand ads (freeze mid-jump or mid-sprint)<br>→ Fashion/beauty (freeze on the styling moment)<br>→ Hero ads where you want a memorable visual hook

## Workflow 4 — Motion Transfer Workflow
**Time:** Background processing. You give it 2 inputs, get 1 output.
You upload a character image and a reference video. Your character picks up the exact motion from the reference — including expression, body language, micro-movements.
### What you input:
→ Character image (the persona you want animated)<br>→ Reference video (the motion/expression you want transferred)
### What you get back:
→ Your character performing the exact motion from the reference<br>→ Expression, body language, and micro-movements transferred<br>→ Identity locked across the full clip
### When this matters:
→ AI influencer accounts where you need consistent character motion across 50+ posts<br>→ Character-based content series<br>→ When you have a great motion reference but need to swap the actor<br>→ Creative direction work where you want specific gestures or expressions
### Pro tip:
The reference video drives everything. If your reference has cluttered backgrounds or fast cuts, the transfer gets messy. Use clean reference videos with single-subject framing for the cleanest output.

## Workflow 5 — Short Film Creator
**Time:** 30 minutes from idea to finished video with music.
Most ambitious workflow in the library. Start with 1 idea. The workflow builds out the scenes, generates the visuals, produces the actual video, and adds appropriate music.
### What you input:

### What you get back:
→ Full scene breakdown<br>→ Generated visuals for every scene<br>→ Final assembled video<br>→ Music track matched to the brand voice
### Where this fits:
→ Weekly brand content for organic pages<br>→ Founder-led brand storytelling<br>→ Multi-format campaigns where you need volume across formats<br>→ Consistent video output at the cadence platforms now reward (2-3 posts/day)
### The honest limitation:
This workflow won't replace film directors with specific creative vision for narrative projects. That's not the point. It's for operators who need consistent video output across formats — week after week — without rebuilding the pipeline every time.

### The 30-variation pattern:
→ 10 hook lines × 3 environments = 30 unique ads from 1 reference image<br>→ Each variation costs $0.60-$1.10<br>→ 30 ads = $18-$33 in production cost<br>→ Total time: 60-90 minutes including waiting for renders
### Hook variation prompt:
```javascript
You are a senior performance media buyer. Rewrite
this hook 10 different ways using these proven
formulas:

1. SPECIFIC NUMBER + TIME: "I [ACTION] [NUMBER] in
   [TIMEFRAME]"
2. CONTRARIAN: "Most [TARGET] do [COMMON]. Here's
   what works."
3. PERSONAL RESULT: "Yesterday I [SPECIFIC OUTCOME]"
4. PATTERN INTERRUPT: "Do you understand what
   [EVENT] just did to [PROBLEM]?"
5. IDENTITY CALLOUT: "If you're [IDENTITY] and
   you're still [BAD HABIT], read this."

For each rewrite:
- Tighten to under 12 words
- Front-load the specific number or claim
- Strip generic adjectives

Original hook: [PASTE YOUR HOOK]
Product context: [PRODUCT, ICP, PRICE, BENEFIT]
```

## What NOT to Do
→ Don't skip the variation step. Running 1 ad and hoping it works is the manual mindset. Workflows give you 30 variations for the cost of 1 traditional production. Use that.
→ Don't try to make every workflow perfect on first run. The first output is the baseline. Variations are the iteration. Run 10, kill 8, scale 2.
→ Don't switch personas mid-campaign. The audience builds recognition over 7-14 days. Locking the persona is what compounds attention.
→ Don't use workflows as your only content type. Mix with statics, live-action UGC, and platform-native content. Workflows scale volume; portfolio diversity holds long-term performance.
→ Don't ignore the cinematic scene workflow because it feels too premium. Brand spots run alongside direct response ads. Both compound.
→ Don't run all 5 workflows on day 1. Pick the 1 that maps to your bottleneck (UGC ads if you need volume, short film if you need consistent organic content) and master it before adding the next.
