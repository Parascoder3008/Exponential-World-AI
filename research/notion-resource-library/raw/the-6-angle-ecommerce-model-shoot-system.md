## ⚙️ The Hidden Mechanism — Why 1 Composite Grid Beats 6 Separate Generations
Every standard AI ecommerce shoot workflow has the same broken loop:
1. Operator generates the eye-level hero shot in GPT Image 2
2. Tries to regenerate the same model from a low angle
3. Model face drifts (different person)
4. Tries again — drifts again
5. Repeats for all 6 angles, ending with 6 inconsistent faces
6. Operator either accepts the inconsistency or hand-picks the closest matches (often 30-50% reshoot rate)
The model isn't bad at any single angle. It's bad at **maintaining identity** when each generation starts fresh.
The 6-angle composite fix is structural: instead of generating 6 separate images, you generate 1 composite image with all 6 panels rendered simultaneously. GPT Image 2 treats the composite as a single composition, which means:
→ Same model identity locks across all 6 panels (1 image = 1 face reference)<br>→ Same outfit holds across all 6 panels (1 image = 1 garment reference)<br>→ Same lighting style holds (1 image = 1 lighting setup)<br>→ Same color grade holds (1 image = 1 grade)<br>→ Same studio background holds (1 image = 1 setting)<br>→ Panel labels render correctly (1 image = 1 typography pass)
Then you extract each panel as a standalone asset for your product page, ads, lookbook, and social.
The fix is not better individual generations. The fix is **rendering all 6 angles as 1 composite** — the thing that lets the same model + outfit hold across every camera angle without drift.

## ⚙️ The 6 Camera Angles Explained
Each angle has a specific commercial use. Match the angle to the asset's role.
### Eye Level
The default. Camera at the model's eye height. Balanced, neutral, conversational.
**Best for:**
- Product page hero shots (ecommerce default)
- Email marketing hero banners
- Catalog spreads
**Why it works:** mirrors how a customer would see the garment in person. No editorial drama. Just clarity.
### Low Angle
Camera below the subject looking up. The model appears taller, more confident, more dominant.
**Best for:**
- Brand campaign ad creative (paid social)
- Lookbook hero pages
- Wholesale brand presentations
**Why it works:** the upward angle creates physical authority. Subject occupies more visual real estate. Premium positioning signal.
### High Angle
Camera above looking down. Intimate, soft, accessible. The model looks slightly up at the camera.
**Best for:**
- Editorial lookbook spreads
- Instagram grid (especially for warm brand voices)
- Magazine-style email hero shots
**Why it works:** the downward angle creates closeness. Reads as personal, less commercial. Good for warm-tone brand voices.
### Dutch Angle
Camera tilted 15-20 degrees on the horizontal. Frame is diagonal instead of flat.
**Best for:**
- Dynamic paid social ad creative
- TikTok thumbnail images
- Youth-targeted brand campaigns
**Why it works:** the diagonal composition creates implied motion. Reads as energetic, youthful, modern. Drives 2x scroll-stop on platforms calibrated for kinetic imagery.
### Bird's Eye View
Extreme top-down. Camera directly above the model, subject looks up at the lens.
**Best for:**
- Flat-lay alternative on product pages
- Lifestyle Instagram grid posts
- Aspirational lookbook spreads
**Why it works:** the overhead perspective shows the full silhouette + context. Useful when the garment's full proportions matter (long dresses, outerwear).
### Worm's Eye View
Extreme low ground perspective. Camera at floor level, dramatic upward shot.
**Best for:**
- Detail-focus paid ads (focus on shoes, lower garment, accessories)
- Editorial lookbook drama pages
- Streetwear / athleisure brand campaigns
**Why it works:** the extreme low angle creates monumental scale. Subject reads as larger-than-life. Strong campaign + manifesto energy.

## ⚙️ The Model Identity Lock Mechanic
This is the trick that keeps the face consistent across all 6 panels. Without it, you get 6 different faces.
The mechanic has 3 layers:
**Layer 1: Hyper-specific model description**
Don't write "woman in her 20s." Write the full model spec (age range + skin tone + hair color + hair length + hair texture + face description + body type + vibe). The more specific the description, the more anchored the identity.
**Layer 2: Explicit lock language**
Inside the prompt, repeat "identity must be consistent across all 6 panels — same person, same face, no drift" twice. The repetition reinforces the lock.
**Layer 3: Composite generation (not separate generations)**
This is the structural unlock. Generating all 6 panels in 1 composite forces GPT Image 2 to treat them as a single composition with shared identity. Generating 6 separate images guarantees drift.
The 3 layers combined produce 90-95% identity consistency across the composite. Without all 3, expect 50-70% consistency (1-2 panels drift per generation).

## ⚙️ The Post-Processing Workflow (Figma + CapCut)
After the composite renders, polish in Figma + CapCut.
### Figma — Panel Extraction (3 minutes)
1. Import the composite PNG into Figma
2. Use the slice tool to crop each panel (6 individual exports)
3. Resize each panel to standard product page dimensions:
	- Square: 1080×1080 (Meta feed, Instagram grid)
	- Portrait: 1080×1350 (Instagram portrait, Pinterest)
	- Vertical: 1080×1920 (Reels, TikTok, Stories)
	- Hero landscape: 1920×1080 (web hero banners)
4. Export each as 4K PNG with transparent background option (for layering)
### CapCut — Polish (3 minutes)
For panels going to ads or video product pages:
1. Import each panel
2. Apply brand-aligned color grade (warm cinematic for lifestyle brands, clean editorial for premium)
3. Subtle vignette (2-3%) for premium feel
4. Brand typography overlay (logo + tagline)
5. If video version: extend with the optional Seedance 2.0 step (Section 6)
Total post-processing: 6-8 minutes per composite.

## ⚙️ What NOT to Do
→ Don't skip the model archetype lock. Define your brand's go-to archetype once. Reuse across every outfit drop.
→ Don't generate 6 separate images and expect identity to hold. The composite generation IS the mechanism.
→ Don't write generic model descriptions. "Woman in her 20s" produces drift. Hyper-specific descriptions produce locks.
→ Don't skip the negative prompt. Without it, expect AI artifacts in 20-30% of generations.
→ Don't deploy a composite that fails the 6-point QC. Identity drift on the product page kills brand consistency.
→ Don't extract panels at low resolution. Each panel should be 4K (≈1024×1024 minimum after cropping).
→ Don't use this system for editorial brand campaigns. Real shoots still win for hero brand pieces. Use this for ecommerce + ads production.
→ Don't ignore the panel angle matrix. Eye Level for product page works because customers expect it. Dutch Angle for product page reads as "weird" — save it for ads.
→ Don't rotate too many model archetypes within 1 quarter. Audience pattern recognition compounds. Stick with 1-2 archetypes per drop season.
→ Don't expect 100% identity hold. 90-95% is the realistic ceiling. Plan for 1-2 in 20 generations needing a regenerate.
→ Don't write outfit descriptions that contradict the model archetype. "Conservative blazer on a streetwear-vibe model" creates ambiguity. Match outfit aesthetic to archetype.
