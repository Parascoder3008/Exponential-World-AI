## ⚙️ The Hidden Mechanism — Why a Production Board Beats Scene-by-Scene Generation
Every standard AI cinematic workflow has the same broken loop:
1. Write 15 separate scene prompts in a doc
2. Generate scene 1 in GPT Image 2 → check identity, lighting, framing
3. Generate scene 2 → identity drifts from scene 1
4. Re-generate scene 2 with stronger identity locks → lighting drifts
5. Repeat across 15 scenes, each generation losing context on the previous ones
6. Drop all 15 into Seedance individually → motion style drifts across scenes
7. Stitch in editor → 2 hours of color correction to mask the drift
Total: 4-6 hours per ad. Output: brittle consistency. Brand-feel: inconsistent.
The Production Board fix is structural: instead of generating 15 scenes individually, you generate **1 storyboard image containing all 15 scenes at once**, structured as a 5x3 grid with header, music journey waveform, and camera movement guide. GPT Image 2 treats the entire grid as a single composition, which means:
→ Lighting style locks across all 15 frames (1 image = 1 light setup)<br>→ Color grading locks (1 image = 1 grade)<br>→ Character identity locks (1 image = 1 reference)<br>→ Product appearance locks (1 image = 1 product render)<br>→ Brand typography locks (header + end frame on the same canvas)<br>→ Music timing locks (waveform on the same canvas as the visuals)
Then Seedance 2.0 receives the production board as the locked reference, plus a 15-scene prompt that repeats every frame's CAM, SFX, MUSIC notes verbatim. Seedance animates each frame into a 2-second clip with consistent motion style, then auto-stitches them into a 30-second cinematic ad.
The fix is not faster generation. The fix is **1-image multi-scene context** — the thing that lets cinematic output stay coherent across 15 scenes without manual correction.

## ⚙️ Step 3 — Animate with Seedance 2.0
Drop the production board into Seedance 2.0 as the locked reference. Paste the master Seedance animation prompt (Section 6). Each of the 15 scene's CAM, SFX, MUSIC notes is repeated verbatim in the Seedance prompt, which gives the model frame-by-frame direction.
Seedance generates each scene as a 2-second clip (15 scenes × 2s = 30 seconds total) then auto-stitches them into 1 continuous video. Total animation time: 4-6 minutes for the full 30-second ad.
### Critical Seedance constraints:
Add the following 3 negative-prompt lines to every Seedance generation:
```javascript
- no camera gear visible in shots
- no drone or rig visible in shots
- no talking heads, no lip sync needed
```
Without these, Seedance defaults to showing crew rigs in 1-2 frames, which breaks the cinematic illusion.

## ⚙️ What NOT to Do
→ Don't generate scenes individually. The 1-image production board IS the mechanism. Generating 15 separate scenes loses identity + lighting consistency.
→ Don't skip the 7-point QC check before Seedance. Animating a flawed storyboard wastes Seedance credits.
→ Don't forget the negative-prompt constraints in Seedance. Without "no camera gear, no drone, no talking heads," Seedance defaults to showing crew rigs in 1-2 frames.
→ Don't use the same music journey on every ad. Vary INTRO duration, DROP timing, OUTRO style across ads to prevent audio fatigue.
→ Don't run cinematic ads as cold-audience hook-rate tests. Hold rate + retargeting is their strength. Use UGC + broadcast realism for cold-audience scroll-stop.
→ Don't deploy without 3 aspect ratios. Export 9:16 for Reels/TikTok, 16:9 for YouTube, 1:1 for Meta feed. The same cinematic ad can run on 3 platforms with the polish step.
→ Don't ignore reverse-direction scenes from Seedance. 1-2 scenes per generation will render in reverse. Fix in CapCut by extracting audio, reversing the clip, re-layering audio.
→ Don't run cinematic ads in isolation. They compound with UGC and broadcast realism — together they cover cold, warm, and brand layers. Alone they only carry the brand layer.
→ Don't change the 15-frame structure on the first 10 productions. The structure (opening → hero → details → lifestyle → action → mood → final → branding) is calibrated. Deviate only after 10 cycles of data.
→ Don't skip the music journey waveform on the production board. The waveform locks audio timing in Seedance. Without it, Seedance freelances on audio cuts and breaks the pacing.
→ Don't use this for talking-head UGC. The Production Board is for cinematic product films. UGC with dialog should run through MakeUGC or Maxfusion instead.

## ⚙️ Troubleshooting
<table header-row="true">
<tr>
<td>Problem</td>
<td>Fix</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Production board frames look inconsistent</td>
<td>The bracket fields were too vague. Be hyper-specific about product details (colors, materials, finish). Regenerate.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Header text comes back garbled</td>
<td>GPT Image 2 sometimes mangles typography. Regenerate, or paste the header text manually in CapCut as a text overlay.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Seedance ignores the production board reference</td>
<td>Re-upload at higher resolution. Lock identity strength to 0.9+. Specify "use this storyboard as the locked visual reference" in the prompt.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>1-2 scenes render in reverse direction</td>
<td>This is standard Seedance behavior. Fix in CapCut: extract audio → reverse video → re-layer audio. 30 seconds per fix.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Camera gear visible in some frames</td>
<td>Add stronger negatives: "no tripod, no boom mic, no lighting rig, no crew member visible." Re-run those scenes.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Color grading drifts between scenes</td>
<td>Apply a unifying LUT in CapCut after Seedance export. Free options: free LUT packs from [Lutify.me](http://lutify.me/), IWLTBAP.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Music timing doesn't match the waveform</td>
<td>The waveform in the production board is a visual reference. You still need to layer the actual music in CapCut. Match the timing manually.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Generation takes too long</td>
<td>The production board generation should be 60-90s. If it's hanging, your prompt is too long. Trim the bracket fields.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Output looks cheap, not cinematic</td>
<td>The "Visual quality" closing paragraph in the master prompt is critical. Don't trim it. Reinforce "premium agency presentation style, high-end brand pitch deck quality."</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Costs add up faster than expected</td>
<td>Cinematic ads cost more per generation than UGC (more compute). Budget 5-10 generations per week, not per day.</td>
</tr>
</table>
