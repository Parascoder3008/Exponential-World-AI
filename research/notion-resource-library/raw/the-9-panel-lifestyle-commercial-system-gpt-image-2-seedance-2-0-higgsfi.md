## ⚙️ The Hidden Mechanism — Why 9 Panels in 3x3 Beats Sequential Scene Generation
Every standard AI cinematic workflow generates scenes one at a time. Result: identity drifts. Lighting shifts. Wardrobe details change. Color grading wanders. Each generation loses context on the previous ones, and the operator spends 2 hours masking the drift in post.
The 9-panel 3x3 storyboard fix is structural: instead of generating 9 scenes individually, you generate **1 image containing all 9 scenes at once**, arranged in a 3x3 grid with shot titles, visual notes, action notes, and SFX/music direction.
GPT Image 2 treats the entire grid as a single composition, which means:
→ **Character identity locks across all 9 panels** (1 image = 1 reference)<br>→ **Wardrobe + accessories lock** (the orange headphones and blue cassette stay identical across every frame)<br>→ **Lighting style locks** (warm golden sunlight holds across every shot)<br>→ **Color grading locks** (1 image = 1 grade)<br>→ **Product identity locks** (the same blue cassette across all 9 frames)<br>→ **Brand aesthetic locks** (retro 90s music commercial vibe holds throughout)
Then Seedance 2.0 receives the 9-panel storyboard as the locked reference, plus a 9-scene prompt that repeats every panel's title, visual, action, and SFX/music notes verbatim. Seedance animates each panel into a \\~1.6-second clip, then auto-stitches them into a 15-second cinematic.
The fix is not faster generation. The fix is **1-image multi-scene context** — the thing that lets cinematic output stay coherent across 9 scenes without manual color correction.
Why 9 (not 15)? 15-panel is for 30-second premium cinematics with action-heavy storytelling. 9-panel is the tighter format for 15-second lifestyle/music commercials — perfect for fashion drops, beverage launches, product lifestyle ads, and music-driven spots. Faster generation. Tighter narrative arc. Better fit for Reels/TikTok crossposting.

### Critical Seedance constraints:
Add these to every Seedance generation:
```javascript
- no visible camera gear or filming rigs
- no on-screen text overlays during animation
- no abrupt cuts between panels (smooth transitions)
- maintain character + wardrobe + product identity
  across all 9 panel transitions
- audio: layer music matching the era/aesthetic
  specified in the storyboard
```
Without these, Seedance defaults to showing crew rigs, abrupt cuts, or text overlays that break the cinematic illusion.

## ⚙️ What NOT to Do
→ Don't generate scenes individually. The 1-image 9-panel storyboard IS the mechanism. Generating 9 separate scenes loses identity + wardrobe consistency.
→ Don't skip the 6-point QC check before Seedance. Animating a flawed storyboard wastes Seedance credits.
→ Don't forget the Seedance constraints (no camera gear, no overlays, smooth transitions). Without them, Seedance defaults to showing rigs or adding random text.
→ Don't pick a vertical aesthetic that doesn't match your brand. Retro 90s feels wrong for a clinical supplement brand. Editorial luxury feels wrong for an athletic brand. Match aesthetic to brand positioning.
→ Don't run 9-panel commercials as cold-audience hook-rate tests. Hold rate + brand recall + warm retargeting is their strength. Use UGC + broadcast realism for cold-audience scroll-stop.
→ Don't deploy without 3 aspect ratios. Export 16:9 for YouTube, 9:16 for Reels/TikTok, 1:1 for Meta feed. Same commercial, 3 platforms, 1 polish step.
→ Don't ignore reverse-direction frames from Seedance. 1 panel per generation might render with wrong motion direction. Fix in CapCut: extract audio, reverse video, re-layer audio.
→ Don't change the 9-panel structure on the first 10 productions. The structure (wide → close-up → low angle → side profile → medium → detail → over-the-shoulder → emotional close-up → hero + logo) is calibrated for narrative arc. Deviate only after 10 cycles of data.
→ Don't use this for talking-head UGC. The 9-panel format is for cinematic lifestyle commercials without dialog. Use Arcads or MakeUGC for talking-head UGC.
→ Don't skip the SFX/MUSIC notes on the storyboard panels. They lock audio timing in Seedance. Without them, Seedance freelances on cuts and breaks the pacing.
→ Don't use the same character/wardrobe combo across consecutive weeks. Audience pattern recognition compounds, but after 4-5 commercials with the same look, fatigue sets in. Rotate the character archetype every 2-3 weeks.
