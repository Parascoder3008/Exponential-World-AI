## ⚙️ The Hidden Mechanism — Why 2-View Character Sheets Are the Bottleneck
Every standard AI cinematic workflow has the same failure mode:
1. Operator generates a character sheet with 2 views (face close-up + full body on grey)
2. Feeds sheet to Seedance for scene 1 render
3. Scene 1 looks great
4. Feeds sheet to Seedance for scene 2 with different emotion
5. Character face changes because the sheet had ONLY neutral expression to reference
6. Feeds sheet to Seedance for scene 3 with different body angle
7. Character body proportions shift because sheet had ONLY straight-on full-body
8. 15 seconds of animation = character drift by beat 3
The fix is structural: the character identity sheet needs to hold every angle + expression + detail the beat sequence will require. Amateur sheets have 2 views. Production-grade sheets have 16+ views + expressions + detail insets.
When Seedance has 5 body angles + 7 facial expressions + detail insets to reference, it can render ANY beat while locking the exact character identity. When it has only 2 views, it's forced to guess at the angles + emotions it hasn't seen.
The fix is not "better lock strength." The fix is **production-grade character sheet with 16+ reference views** — the thing that turns AI cinematic from a demo experiment into a shippable production format.

## ⚙️ Step 1 — The Production-Grade Character Identity Sheet
The single most important asset in the entire workflow. 2-view sheets fail at 5-second mark. Production-grade sheets hold through 15+ seconds.
### The 4-Section Identity Sheet Layout
**Section 1 — Full Body Turnaround (5 angles)**<br>→ Front view<br>→ 3/4 left view<br>→ Side view<br>→ 3/4 right view<br>→ Back view
**Section 2 — Head Turnaround (6 angles)**<br>→ Front<br>→ 3/4 left<br>→ Left profile<br>→ 3/4 right<br>→ Right profile<br>→ Back of head
**Section 3 — Facial Expressions (7 emotions)**<br>→ Neutral (default)<br>→ Happy (subtle smile, warm)<br>→ Sad (downcast, contemplative)<br>→ Curious (raised brow, engaged)<br>→ Angry (subtle frown, tense)<br>→ Surprised (raised brow, wide eye)<br>→ Determined (focused, jaw set)
**Section 4 — Detail Insets (4 elements)**<br>→ Hair detail (close-up of texture + color)<br>→ Clothing fabric (close-up of material)<br>→ Shoes (close-up of footwear)<br>→ Accessories (close-up of jewelry / watch / bag)
**Section 5 — Character Metadata (top of sheet)**<br>→ Character name / archetype label<br>→ Age<br>→ Gender<br>→ Height<br>→ Build

## ⚙️ Step 2 — The Scene-by-Scene Story System
15 seconds. 3-5 beats. Each beat references a specific facial expression + body angle from the identity sheet.
### The 3-Beat Structure (Shortest)
**Beat 1 (0-5s) — Establishing**<br>→ Character introduced in setting<br>→ Face: neutral or curious<br>→ Body: 3/4 left OR straight-on<br>→ Camera: slow establishing move (push-in or lateral)
**Beat 2 (5-10s) — Interaction**<br>→ Character interacts with environment / prop / product<br>→ Face: shifts to appropriate emotion (happy / determined / curious)<br>→ Body: reference matching angle from sheet<br>→ Camera: closer, intimate framing
**Beat 3 (10-15s) — Resolution**<br>→ Character resolves the moment (smile, glance, settle)<br>→ Face: final emotional beat (happy / neutral)<br>→ Body: reference final angle<br>→ Camera: settle into medium close-up
### The 4-Beat Structure (Balanced)
**Beat 1 (0-4s)** — Establishing<br>**Beat 2 (4-8s)** — Rising interaction<br>**Beat 3 (8-12s)** — Emotional peak<br>**Beat 4 (12-15s)** — Resolution
### The 5-Beat Structure (Richest)
**Beat 1 (0-3s)** — Establishing (wide/setting)<br>**Beat 2 (3-6s)** — Approach (character enters focus)<br>**Beat 3 (6-9s)** — Interaction (character engages)<br>**Beat 4 (9-12s)** — Reveal (emotional shift or product moment)<br>**Beat 5 (12-15s)** — Resolution (settle into ending)
Match beat count to story complexity. Simple moments = 3 beats. Emotional arcs = 4-5 beats.
### The Story Structure Prompt (Claude Fable 5 Skill)

## ⚙️ Step 3 — The Seedance 2.0 Beat-Animation Protocol
Feed each beat into Seedance 2.0 separately. Attach the character identity sheet. Lock at 0.97.
### Per-Beat Render Setup
1. Open Higgsfield or Replicate → select Seedance 2.0 Pro
2. Attach character identity sheet as reference (`@character_[NAME]` from Cinema Studio)
3. Set identity lock strength: 0.97
4. Paste the beat prompt from Fable 5 skill output
5. Add these standard preservation lines:
```javascript
Preserve character identity exactly from the
attached identity sheet. Reference:
- Body angle: [SPECIFIC ANGLE from sheet]
- Facial expression: [SPECIFIC EMOTION from
  sheet]

Lighting: match previous beat's lighting arc.
Duration: [SECONDS from beat structure]
Aspect ratio: [MATCH TARGET]
Resolution: 4K
```
1. Render
Render time per beat: 45-90 seconds.<br>Total render time for 5-beat cinematic: 5-8 minutes (parallel where possible).
Cost per beat: $0.35-$0.80 depending on duration.<br>Total cost per 15-second cinematic: \\~$2-$4 in Seedance renders.
### The Continuity Preservation Between Beats
The trickiest part is making beat 2 feel like a natural continuation of beat 1 rather than a fresh scene.
**The lighting-arc rule**: Each beat's lighting must match the arc established in beat 1. If beat 1 has warm morning window light from left, beat 2 should preserve that direction + color temperature.
**The setting rule**: Same physical location across all beats. Only framing varies (wide → medium → close-up).
**The wardrobe rule**: Same outfit across all beats. If the story requires a wardrobe change, add a transition beat.
**The emotional-shift rule**: Emotions transition smoothly. Neutral → Curious → Happy is smooth. Neutral → Angry → Happy is jarring.
### The Post-Render QC Pass
After all beats render, watch the assembled 15 seconds:
→ Character face identical across all beats? ✓<br>→ Character body proportions consistent? ✓<br>→ Wardrobe unchanged (unless deliberate transition)? ✓<br>→ Lighting arc smooth? ✓<br>→ Emotional progression believable? ✓
If any beat fails, re-render only that beat with tightened locks or clearer angle/expression reference.

## ⚙️ The Character Continuity Audit Protocol
Before assembly, run this check on your beat renders.
### The 5-Point Continuity Checklist
**Point 1 — Face Identity**<br>Compare beat 1 face to beat 5 face. Same person? Same eyes? Same nose? Same mouth?<br>→ Pass: proceed<br>→ Fail: identify which beat drifted, re-render with tighter identity lock (0.98)
**Point 2 — Body Proportions**<br>Compare full-body shots across beats. Same height? Same build? Same posture character?<br>→ Pass: proceed<br>→ Fail: identity sheet may lack proportion clarity — regenerate sheet with more explicit build description
**Point 3 — Wardrobe Consistency**<br>Same outfit across all beats?<br>→ Pass: proceed<br>→ Fail: wardrobe reference lost — attach clothing fabric detail inset separately
**Point 4 — Setting Consistency**<br>Same physical space (framing varies but space is same)?<br>→ Pass: proceed<br>→ Fail: setting drifted — use location Element from Cinema Studio + reference in every beat
**Point 5 — Lighting Arc**<br>Smooth lighting transition across beats?<br>→ Pass: proceed<br>→ Fail: adjust lighting language per beat to match arc

## ⚙️ Step 4 — The CapCut Micro-Polish (5 Minutes)
Assemble the beats, add the cinematic slice-of-life finish.
### 5-Minute Polish Protocol
**Minutes 0-1**: Import<br>→ Drag all 3-5 Seedance beat outputs into CapCut in beat order<br>→ Verify aspect ratio matches deploy target
**Minute 1**: Cut Style<br>→ Hard cuts between beats (NO transitions — cinematic slice-of-life uses hard cuts)<br>→ Verify total runtime is exactly 15 seconds
**Minute 2**: Color Grade<br>→ Apply subtle cinematic grade using CapCut Auto → Cinematic or Neutral<br>→ For warm slice-of-life: shift slightly toward amber<br>→ For cool slice-of-life: shift slightly toward teal<br>→ Match all beats to same grade
**Minute 3**: Audio Layer<br>→ Add subtle ambient audio matching the setting (kitchen morning / bathroom running water / cafe murmur / office quiet)<br>→ Very light instrumental if brand permits — NO lyrics<br>→ Volume: ambient at -18 to -12 dB
**Minute 4**: Captions (Optional)<br>→ If your slice-of-life has any dialogue, add native captions<br>→ Font: Inter Bold or platform-native<br>→ Style: bottom-third, white with black stroke<br>→ For pure silent slice-of-life: skip captions entirely
**Minute 5**: Export<br>→ 4K resolution<br>→ 30fps<br>→ File naming: `[BRAND]_SLICE-OF-LIFE_[CONCEPT]_v1.mp4`
Total polish time: 5 minutes once template saved.

## ⚙️ What NOT to Do
→ Don't use 2-view character sheets. They fail at 5-second mark. Production-grade sheet with all 4 sections is non-negotiable.
→ Don't skip the emotional expression section on the character sheet. If your story has any emotional arc, Seedance needs the expression references.
→ Don't try to lock identity via prompt language alone. The visual reference in the sheet is what locks. Prompt language is secondary.
→ Don't add music with lyrics. Slice-of-life works best with ambient audio or subtle instrumental.
→ Don't cut with transitions. Hard cuts are the cinematic slice-of-life language. Transitions read as amateur.
→ Don't animate scenes longer than 5 seconds per beat. Beyond 5s, drift risk increases. Break long beats into 2 beats.
→ Don't try to include dialogue in slice-of-life. The format works because it's non-verbal. Dialogue kills the intimacy.
→ Don't shoot beats in random order. Render in narrative sequence so continuity issues show up chronologically.
→ Don't skip the continuity audit protocol. Every beat gets a pass before assembly.
→ Don't measure slice-of-life on CTR. Measure hold rate + view-through + saves + brand recall.
→ Don't run slice-of-life without brand-safe locations. If your character is in a bathroom or bedroom, verify the setting matches your brand's target aesthetic.
→ Don't include product prominently in every beat. Product appears incidentally, never as pitch. This is intimate character content, not a product ad.
