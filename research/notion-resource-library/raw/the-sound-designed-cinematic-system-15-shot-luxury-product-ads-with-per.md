## ⚙️ The Hidden Mechanism — Why Per-Shot SFX Direction Beats Music-Only Cinematic Briefs
Every standard AI cinematic workflow has the same broken loop on audio:
1. Operator generates a 15-shot cinematic with Claude + GPT Image 2 + Seedance
2. Briefs music beats at the storyboard level ("INTRO / BUILD / DROP / OUTRO")
3. Layers a generic music track in CapCut
4. Ships an ad with music but no sound design — no whoosh on the product reveal, no lace tension on the close-up, no impact thud on the slow-mo
5. Output looks cinematic but feels flat because audio is doing 30% of what real cinema audio does
Real cinema audio has 3 layers:
→ **Music** (the emotional spine)<br>→ **Ambient** (the environmental context — city hum, room tone)<br>→ **SFX** (the specific sound design — whoosh, crinkle, impact, snap, tone)
Most AI cinematic workflows brief only music. SFX gets either ignored or freelanced by the model. Result: ads that look cinematic but sound generic.
The per-shot SFX fix is structural: brief the SFX at the storyboard level alongside CAM direction. Every panel in the storyboard includes a specific SFX call (lid whoosh, tissue crinkle, water splash, impact thud). Seedance receives the SFX cue at the correct frame. CapCut layering happens at the correct timestamp.
The fix is not better music. The fix is **per-shot SFX direction encoded at the storyboard level** — the thing that lets AI cinematic ads feel intentional vs feel like AI motion.

## ⚙️ The Phantom-Cam Slow-Mo Technique
Shot 10 (Phantom-cam ultra slow-motion) is the cinematic peak of the 15-shot arc. The Phantom-cam reference is the Phantom Flex 4K — the real cinema camera used for high-end commercial slow-motion (water droplet impacts, fabric movement, shoe lace tension).
To execute the Phantom-cam shot in Seedance:
```javascript
Shot 10 — Phantom-cam ultra slow-motion impact

Camera: Phantom-cam low angle, ground-level
perspective, looking up at subject. Render as
if shot on a Phantom Flex 4K high-speed cinema
camera at 1000fps, played back at 24fps
(40x slow-motion).

Action: [SPECIFIC IMPACT MOMENT — water
splash on shoe, lace snap into place,
clasp closing, bottle dropping into liquid,
etc.]

Duration: 1.5 seconds of footage (which
represents 60 seconds of real-time impact
captured at high-speed)

Visual treatment:
- Ultra slow-mo with visible motion blur
  trails
- Crystalline detail (each individual water
  droplet, fabric fiber, particle visible)
- High dynamic range, premium color grade
- Single sharp focus point on impact

Audio cue:
- Impact thud at frame 1 of the shot (the
  peak moment of impact)
- Followed by deep bass reverberation that
  carries through the slow-mo footage
- Music drops to silence then re-enters at
  shot 11

The Phantom-cam shot IS the cinematic peak.
Every other shot leads up to it.
```
This shot type is what separates "AI cinematic ad" from "premium production cinematic." Spend extra credits on regenerating shot 10 until the impact moment lands perfectly. The rest of the cinematic builds toward this moment.

## ⚙️ What NOT to Do
→ Don't skip the per-shot SFX direction. Without it, you're back to music-only generic cinematic.
→ Don't use the same SFX across multiple shots. Variety is what makes the sound design feel intentional vs lazy.
→ Don't skip the deliberate silence moments. Shots 3, 11, 14 use silence as a tool. Filling them with SFX defeats the dramatic contrast.
→ Don't under-mix the SFX. SFX should sit at -12dB to -6dB — above the music (-18dB) so the audience hears them as intentional design.
→ Don't over-mix the SFX either. SFX above -6dB starts feeling cartoonish. Find the sweet spot.
→ Don't deploy the Phantom-cam shot (shot 10) without a strong impact SFX. The visual peak needs the audio peak.
→ Don't use generic stock music. Match the music to the brand tone (orchestral for refined, electronic for kinetic, hip-hop for streetwear).
→ Don't ignore the per-vertical SFX swaps. A sneaker ad with watch clasp SFX feels wrong. Use the vertical-specific library.
→ Don't expect Seedance to generate native SFX perfectly. Use Claude's audio cue map as your CapCut layering instruction sheet — manual SFX layering at the right timestamps is more reliable than model-generated SFX.
→ Don't run sound-designed cinematics on TikTok cold audiences for hook rate testing. The format is for hold rate + brand recall. Use UGC for cold-audience hook tests.
→ Don't replace your existing cinematic workflows. Sound-designed is the premium tier. Standard cinematic is the volume tier. Run both.

## ⚙️ Troubleshooting
<table header-row="true">
<tr>
<td>Problem</td>
<td>Fix</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>SFX doesn't land at the right frame</td>
<td>Claude's audio cue timestamp wrong. Verify the cue map matches the visual event in the generated video. Adjust manually in CapCut.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Sound design feels generic</td>
<td>Per-shot SFX not specific enough. Re-run Claude with explicit "use vertical-specific SFX library — lace tension for shoes, atomizer spray for fragrance, etc."</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Music drowns the SFX</td>
<td>Lower music to -18dB. Raise SFX to -10 or -8dB. SFX must punch through the music mix.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>SFX feels too loud / cartoonish</td>
<td>SFX above -6dB. Lower to -12 to -10dB range.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Silence at shot 3 feels awkward, not dramatic</td>
<td>The visual at shot 3 isn't strong enough to carry silence. Make sure shot 3 is the hero static — clean composition that earns the silence.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Phantom-cam shot impact doesn't hit</td>
<td>SFX not heavy enough. Use a bass-heavy impact thud, not a generic impact. Layer with sub-bass reverb tail.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Whoosh SFX overused across shots</td>
<td>Variety problem. Replace duplicate whoosh calls with category-specific SFX (snap, click, rumble, splash).</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Generation cost spikes</td>
<td>Per-shot regenerations add up. Run the full 15-shot in 1 batch when possible. Only regenerate failed shots individually.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Brand typography at shot 15 garbled</td>
<td>GPT Image 2 mangles typography. Paste the brand logo + tagline as a CapCut overlay in post — don't rely on the model to render brand text.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Audio cue map doesn't match shot durations</td>
<td>Verify each shot's actual duration in Seedance output. Adjust the CapCut SFX timestamps to match.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Costs add up faster than expected</td>
<td>SFX library is a one-time investment (Epidemic Sound subscription \\~$15/month). Generation costs same as standard cinematic. Don't double-source.</td>
</tr>
</table>
