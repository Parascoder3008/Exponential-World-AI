## ⚙️ The Hidden Mechanism — Why an 8-Shot Narrative Board Beats Product-Hero Storyboards
Every standard product-hero storyboard has the same broken loop when you try to do narrative:
1. Operator writes a single-character storyboard for a narrative ad
2. The 2nd character gets introduced late, identity drifts
3. The dialogue beat is rendered as a single static frame instead of a dialogue moment
4. Multi-location movement gets compressed into 1 location
5. The emotional arc flattens because the storyboard didn't lock the arc
6. Output looks like a product demo, not a commercial
Narrative commercials need 5 elements that product-hero formats don't lock:
→ **Character + styling reference** for each character at multiple angles<br>→ **Environment + camera map** showing physical movement through space<br>→ **Dialogue moments** where the audio + character interaction is the visual anchor<br>→ **Mood + keywords block** defining the emotional palette across the arc<br>→ **Cinematography notes** with lens grammar that signals "commercial" not "demo"
The 7-section production board fix is structural: every variable a narrative ad needs gets a dedicated section on the board. The model treats each section as a binding contract. The output one-shots because there's nothing left to guess.
The fix is not better prompts. The fix is **encoding the entire pre-production guide as one image** — character refs, wardrobe, locations, camera map, shot list, mood, audio, cinematography all locked into a single visual scaffold.

## ⚙️ Step 2 — Generate the Product Hero Asset in Grok
The Grok asset trick: render the product as a clean stylized 3D hero before building the production board. Why Grok specifically:
→ Cheap + fast (faster iteration than GPT Image 2 for single-product renders)<br>→ Tight control over branding/typography (better than Seedance's text rendering)<br>→ Output works as a reference image fed back into ChatGPT Image 2's production board
### The Master Grok Product Asset Prompt
```javascript
A high-detail stylized 3D render of [PRODUCT
TYPE] with [HOUSING/CONTAINER COLOR] that says
"[BRAND NAME]" in a [TYPOGRAPHY STYLE — simple
golden font, sans-serif white, etched silver,
etc.], on a [BACKGROUND COLOR] background.

Render style:
- Photorealistic 3D
- Soft studio lighting from camera right
- Subtle reflection on base surface
- Clear product hero composition
- Brand typography sharp and legible
- No additional props, no clutter

Output: 1024x1024 or 1500x1500. Single product
on clean background. Ready for use as a
reference image in downstream storyboard tools.
```
Generate 3-4 variations in Grok. Pick the best by typography clarity + lighting + brand color accuracy. This single asset gets fed back into ChatGPT Image 2 as the product reference.

## ⚙️ Step 5 — Animate with Seedance 2.0 Inside Higgsfield
Drop the production board into Seedance 2.0 inside Higgsfield. Paste the master narrative animation prompt (Section 10). Attach character references + product reference as locked inputs.
Higgsfield's image verification may ask you to confirm rights to use the character likenesses. Confirm if you have rights or used AI-generated characters.
Seedance animates each shot into \\~1.8-second clip (8 shots × 1.875s ≈ 15 seconds total). Auto-stitches with soft cuts. Total animation time: 4-6 minutes.
### Critical narrative animation constraints:
```javascript
- Read the production board as the LOCKED PLAN
- Match every shot's camera/lens, shot size,
  movement, action, and emotion verbatim
- Lock character identity across all 8 shots
  (especially character 2 in shots 6-7)
- Render dialogue beats with visible mouth
  movement + emotion (lipsync where applicable)
- Handle multi-location movement smoothly
  (don't compress to 1 location)
- Maintain product identity across shots 3-4 + 8
- Soft cuts between shots, no abrupt cuts
- No camera gear visible, no crew visible
```

  DIALOGUE]"]

Shot 08 (0:14-0:15): [SHOT 8 BRAND END +

Character 1: lock identity from production

typography distortion.

Multi-location movement:
Render the physical movement through space
following the Environment + Set Design camera
map. Don't compress locations.

Dialogue rendering (shot 6):
Render visible mouth movement matching the
dialogue audio. Natural pauses, breathing,
emotion in face.

Lighting:
Match the Lighting + Mood + Style Notes section
verbatim. Dusk/blue hour for exteriors.
Candlelit warm for interiors.

Audio:
Layer ambient + music per Audio/Tone section.

Constraints:
- No camera gear visible
- No crew or rig visible
- Maintain character identity across all 8
  shots
- Soft cuts between shots
- No abrupt cuts except match cuts on action
- Output: 15 seconds, 16:9 or 9:16, 4K

Cinematography:
- Lens mix per production board (24mm
  establishing, 35-85mm intimacy, macro for
  details)
- Movement smooth and purposeful (tracking,
  steadicam, slight push-ins)

  product closeups

## ⚙️ What NOT to Do
→ Don't skip the Grok product asset step. ChatGPT Image 2 can render products but typography control is weaker. Grok handles brand label typography 3x more reliably.
→ Don't strip any of the 7 production board sections. Each section locks a specific variable narrative ads need.
→ Don't write narrative storyboards in plain English prompts. The 7-section structured board is the contract Seedance reads. Paragraphs revert to product-hero defaults.
→ Don't use this format for product-hero-only ads. Use the 15-frame Production Board or 10-frame Maxfusion one-shot for those. Narrative commercials are for story + dialogue + character interaction.
→ Don't compress multi-location movement into 1 location. The Environment + Camera Map section locks the movement. Verify it rendered correctly.
→ Don't skip the dialogue beat verification. Shot 6 (dialogue beat) is the most fragile. Verify the storyboard shows clear dialogue framing before animating.
→ Don't deploy without all 3 aspect ratios. Export 9:16, 16:9, 1:1. Same narrative ad, 3 platform-native cuts.
→ Don't run the system on dialog-heavy scripts. The 8-shot structure compresses to 15 seconds. 1-3 short dialog beats max. Long dialog breaks the timing.
→ Don't ignore Higgsfield's image rights prompts. If it asks for confirmation, confirm only if you have actual rights. Don't bypass for likenesses you don't own.
→ Don't expect every shot to one-shot perfectly. Narrative animations have higher failure rates than product-hero formats due to identity drift on character 2 + dialogue rendering. Budget 1-2 retries per ad.
→ Don't change the 8-shot structure on the first 5 generations. The arc is calibrated. Deviate after data.

## ⚙️ Troubleshooting

<tr>
<td>Problem</td>
<td>Fix</td>

<td>Reinforce "render visible mouth movement matching the dialogue" in the Seedance prompt for shot 6.</td>

<td>Multi-location compresses to 1 location</td>
<td>Environment + Camera Map section not specific enough. Add explicit location transitions: "Shot 1 outside, Shot 5 transition, Shot 6 interior."</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Brand typography in product hero (shot 3) garbled</td>
<td>Use the Grok product asset (with sharp typography baked in) instead of letting Seedance render typography from scratch.</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Cinematography Notes don't carry into output</td>
<td>Reinforce in Seedance prompt: "Apply cinematography from production board: [PASTE LENS + MOVEMENT + FOCUS + GRADE + STYLE notes verbatim]."</td>

<td>Add the keywords inline in the Seedance prompt as emotional anchors.</td>
