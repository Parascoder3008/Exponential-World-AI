## ⚙️ Step 2 — The LIPSYNC MAP Protocol
The LIPSYNC MAP is the difference between "AI-obvious" and "indistinguishable from real." Here's how to build one for any script.
### Building a LIPSYNC MAP for a 15-Second Script
**Step 1**: Write your finished script (15-second version, \\~35-50 words spoken).
**Step 2**: Record the master audio track. Use Suno for music-video style, ElevenLabs for voice-over, or record yourself if authentic voice is required.
**Step 3**: Play back the audio and mark timestamps for every 0.5-3 second window where new syllables begin.
**Step 4**: For each timestamp window, write out the EXACT lyric or dialogue that occurs, with interjections marked ("Hey!" / "Yeah!" / breath marks).
**Step 5**: Mark instrumental / pause sections explicitly.
### Example LIPSYNC MAP for DTC Founder Ad
**Script**: "I built this brand for one reason. Every product tested. Every batch checked. This isn't just skincare. This is my life's work."
**Audio recording**: 15 seconds total, natural pace.
**LIPSYNC MAP**:
```javascript
0.0-0.3 [breath in, mouth closed]
0.3-2.1 "I built this brand for one reason."
2.1-2.5 [brief pause, mouth closed]
2.5-4.3 "Every product tested."
4.3-4.7 [pause, mouth closed]
4.7-6.5 "Every batch checked."
6.5-7.0 [pause, slight nod]
7.0-9.3 "This isn't just skincare."
9.3-9.8 [pause, direct camera stare]
9.8-13.5 "This is my life's work."
13.5-15.0 [instrumental hold, closing eyes
subtly then look back to camera]
```
Every second is accounted for. Seedance now knows exactly when to move the mouth and when NOT to.
### The Anti-Cutaway Rule
Never cut away from the performer's face mid-word. If a camera cut happens, it must land during an instrumental / pause window. Force this in the SHOT FLOW section by aligning all cuts to LIPSYNC MAP pause markers.
### Instrumental / Pause Length Rules
→ 0.3-0.5 seconds: natural breath / breath-in<br>→ 0.5-1.5 seconds: dramatic pause between phrases<br>→ 1.5-3 seconds: transition beat (usually for camera cut)<br>→ 3+ seconds: only for opening / closing instrumental holds
Match pause length to emotional beat requirement.

## ⚙️ Step 3 — The Performer Identity Lock
Higgsfield's Originals lock performer identity with extreme specificity. Vague descriptors cause drift.
### Weak Identity Descriptor (Drift-Prone)
```javascript
"Woman in her twenties wearing white."
```
Result: face changes between beats, wardrobe morphs, identity broken.
### Strong Identity Descriptor (Higgsfield-Grade)
```javascript
"Cornrow braids, septum ring, statement
earrings, sculptural white designer top, dark
indigo denim, face makeup with subtle bronze
highlight, athletic build with defined shoulders,
mid-height frame, direct steady gaze, olive skin
tone with warm undertone."
```
Result: identity holds across every beat.
### The 10-Descriptor Formula
Every performer identity should include:
1. **Hair**: color + length + style (braids, waves, straight, updo, etc.)
2. **Face features**: distinctive marks (freckles, dimples, moles, scars)
3. **Jewelry**: earrings, nose ring, chain, watch
4. **Wardrobe top**: specific garment description with fabric + color
5. **Wardrobe bottom**: specific garment description with fabric + color
6. **Makeup / grooming**: level and style
7. **Build**: body type + height framing
8. **Presence descriptor**: dominant / soft / warm / edgy
9. **Skin tone**: specific color + undertone
10. **Eye gaze**: direct / averted / dreamy / focused
Fill all 10 for lock-tight identity across a full performance.
### The Face-Only Anchor
For maximum lipsync accuracy, additionally attach a face-only reference (cut from the full-body character sheet):
```javascript
"Face reference attached separately as @face_only_[NAME].jpg.
Use as identity lock at 0.98 strength. Every syllable
articulated on this exact face."
```
This kills mid-performance face drift.

## ⚙️ Step 4 — The Mirrored-Echo Director's Thesis (Adapted for DTC)
Higgsfield's signature technique: 5 dancers in identical wardrobe echoing the performer's poses one beat late — creating a "human delay effect behind her voice." For DTC brands, this adapts to different metaphors.
### Adaptation 1 — Community Echo (Customer Testimonial)
Instead of dancers, use 3-5 diverse customer archetypes standing behind the founder, echoing gestures. Message: "Community behind the founder."
```javascript
Visual world: minimalist studio backdrop.
Founder in center rapping the vocal. 3-5
diverse customer archetypes standing in wedge
formation behind founder, each echoing
founder's gestures 0.5 seconds late. Effect:
community delay effect behind founder's voice.
```
### Adaptation 2 — Product Line Echo (Brand Anthem)
Instead of dancers, use different SKUs or product variants displayed on pedestals behind the performer. Each pedestal has the product rotating slightly out of sync — a "product delay effect."
```javascript
Visual world: white infinity studio. Performer
in center delivering brand anthem. 5 pedestals
in wedge formation behind, each holding a
different SKU from the product line. Each SKU
rotates on pedestal one beat delayed from
performer's gestures. Effect: product line
delay effect echoing behind the founder.
```
### Adaptation 3 — Team Echo (Founder Story)
For founder-led brands with a visible team, use the team echoing behind the founder. Message: "The team backing the vision."
```javascript
Visual world: modern office space or workshop.
Founder in foreground telling their story. 3-5
team members visible in soft background depth,
subtly echoing founder's gestures one beat
late. Effect: team delay effect behind
founder's voice.
```
### Adaptation 4 — Time Echo (Transformation Testimonial)
Instead of dancers, use 3-5 "past versions" of the same character (via multi-face sheet with same character in different stages) echoing behind. Message: "The journey to today."
```javascript
Visual world: minimalist studio. Present-day
customer in center delivering testimonial. 3
past-version silhouettes of the same person
visible behind, each in different life stage,
echoing gestures one beat delayed. Effect:
transformation timeline delay effect.
```
### Adaptation 5 — Impact Echo (Brand Mission Video)
Use environmental echoes (waves, particles, symbolic elements) instead of human dancers. Message: "The ripple effect of the mission."
```javascript
Visual world: minimalist studio with subtle
atmospheric background. Founder in center
delivering mission statement. Subtle wave
particles emanate outward from founder in
rhythmic pulses aligned to vocal beats.
Effect: mission ripple effect behind founder's
voice.
```
### Adaptation 6 — Solo Echo (Standard Talking Head)
For simple talking-head ads without the echo effect. Just clean performer + clean setting.
```javascript
Visual world: minimalist studio or
brand-appropriate location. Performer in
center delivering the vocal directly to camera.
No background dancers, no echo effects. Clean
premium single-performer aesthetic.
```



**For product launch pitches**:
```javascript

aesthetic without AI signatures.
```

### The Anti-Gloss / Anti-Glow Anchors
Add to every prompt regardless of format:
```javascript
Anti-anchors:
- No AI gloss (that plastic-look sheen)
- No AI glow (unnatural light bloom around
  subject)
- No beauty filter smoothing
- No unnaturally saturated colors
- No perfectly stable camera (add subtle
  handheld realism)
- Natural skin texture with visible pores
- Natural lighting imperfections
```
These anti-anchors are what kills the "obviously AI" signal in the first 2 seconds.

## ⚙️ What NOT to Do
→ Don't skip the LIPSYNC MAP. It's the entire mechanism. Free-form scripts without timestamp mapping produce generic mouth movement.
→ Don't use vague identity descriptors. 10-descriptor formula is non-negotiable for identity lock.
→ Don't invent audio. Use finished master track (Audio1). Seedance shouldn't generate new music or vocals.
→ Don't skip the anti-gloss / anti-glow anchors. Without them, AI-gloss appears and viewers scroll past.
